Glossary

The canonical wording for this workspace. Terms are added as lessons introduce them; once a term is here, every lesson uses it this way.

How to use this

Definitions marked [ISTQB] are the syllabus wording — use these in interviews, because they are what the interviewer was trained on. Definitions marked [RST] come from Rapid Software Testing and are deliberately different in emphasis; they are better for explaining why you do something. Where the two disagree, that disagreement is worth understanding rather than resolving.

Risk

Risk
A potential event, hazard, threat, or situation whose occurrence causes an adverse effect. [ISTQB §5.2.1]
Risk likelihood
The probability that the risk occurs. One of the two factors that characterise a risk. [ISTQB §5.2.1]
Risk impact
The harm or consequence that results if the risk occurs. The second of the two factors. Not the same as how dramatic the symptom looks. [ISTQB §5.2.1]
Risk level
The combination of likelihood and impact. Higher levels mean the risk matters more and should be treated first. [ISTQB §5.2.1]
Product risk
A risk to the quality of the product — missing or wrong function, poor performance, insecurity, unusability. This is what generates test cases. [ISTQB §5.2.2]
Project risk
A risk to the project’s ability to deliver — organisational, people, technical or supplier issues affecting schedule, budget or scope. You raise it; you do not test it. [ISTQB §5.2.2]
Risk-based testing
The test approach in which test activities are selected, prioritised and managed based on risk analysis and risk control. [ISTQB §5.2]
Risk analysis
Identifying risks and assessing their level, so that testing effort can be focused and residual risk minimised. Best started early in the development lifecycle. [ISTQB §5.2.3]
Risk control
Acting on analysed risks: mitigation (taking measures that reduce the risk) and monitoring (checking those measures worked, and watching for new risks). [ISTQB §5.2.4]
Residual risk
The risk that remains after mitigation. Testing never reaches zero; naming what you knowingly accepted is part of the job.

Quality and testing

Quality
Value to some person who matters. Quality is a relationship between a product and people, not an attribute you can isolate from human context. [RST]
Testing (purpose of)
To discover the status of the product and any threats to its value, so that clients can make informed decisions about it. [RST]
Testing vs. checking
Checking is the part of testing that can in principle be done by machinery — confirming a specific expectation. Testing requires the tester’s explicit and tacit knowledge. Automation performs checks; it does not test. [RST]

Test design models

Decision table
A table for requirements where combinations of conditions produce different outcomes. Conditions and actions are rows; each feasible combination is a decision-rule column. [ISTQB §4.2.3]
Decision rule
One decision-table column: a unique combination of condition values together with the actions that combination should produce. [ISTQB §4.2.3]
State
The current situation of the system that matters to what it may do next. The same event can produce a different result from a different state. [ISTQB §4.2.4]
State transition
A move from one state to another, initiated by an event and sometimes restricted by a condition. [ISTQB §4.2.4]
Valid transitions coverage
Valid transitions exercised divided by valid transitions identified. One sequence may cover several transitions; visiting every state alone is weaker. [ISTQB §4.2.4]

Test artifacts

Test condition
A testable aspect identified during analysis: a rule, transition, risk, or other coverage target that answers what should be examined. [ISTQB §1.4.1]
Test case
A concrete example that elaborates one or more test conditions into a known starting state, specific inputs or actions, and an observable expected result. [ISTQB §1.4.1, §4.5.3]
Test procedure
Test cases organised into an executable order, including the setup, reset and cleanup needed to run them. [ISTQB §1.4.1]
Traceability
Visible links from the test basis through conditions and cases to results and defects. It supports coverage evaluation, change impact analysis and reporting. [ISTQB §1.4.4]

The seven testing principles

ISTQB CTFL v4.0 §1.3. Worth knowing by number — they get cited that way.

# Principle What it means in practice
1 Testing shows the presence, not the absence, of defects Testing can prove defects exist, never that none remain. It reduces the likelihood of undiscovered defects.
2 Exhaustive testing is impossible Infeasible except in trivial cases — hence techniques, prioritisation and risk-based testing.
3 Early testing saves time and money Defects removed early do not propagate into later work products. Start static and dynamic testing early.
4 Defects cluster together A small number of components hold most of the defects. This is what makes a targeted sample better than a random one.
5 Tests wear out Repeating the same tests finds progressively less. Revise and add tests — though repetition still pays in automated regression.
6 Testing is context dependent No single approach applies universally. The right testing depends on the situation.
7 Absence-of-defects fallacy Verifying requirements and fixing defects does not guarantee a successful or wanted system. Validation matters too.
