Software testing · from first principles

A practical QA course

Short, interactive lessons on software testing. Each takes about ten minutes and leaves you able to do something you couldn’t before.

Who this is for. Anyone moving into software testing, or already testing and wanting the reasoning behind the habits. No coding needed until lesson 24. Every claim is cited to a primary source — mostly the ISTQB Foundation syllabus and Rapid Software Testing, which disagree with each other in useful places.

How to use it

Learning from scratch? Start at lesson 1 and work down. Each lesson leans on the one before. Do the exercises rather than reading past them — the feedback is where the learning is, and the answers you get wrong are worth more than the ones you get right.

Following the 52-task plan? The curriculum lists every task ID and links straight to the lesson that covers it. Each lesson also shows its task ID at the top.

Just need a fact? Skip to the reference cards. They are built for looking things up, not for learning from.

A note on the two voices

This course quotes ISTQB and Rapid Software Testing side by side, and they do not always agree — on what testing is, on how much process helps, on whether testing can be reduced to procedure. That disagreement is left visible on purpose. ISTQB wording is what most interviewers were trained on and is worth knowing precisely; RST is generally the better account of why a practice works. Holding both, and being able to say where you land, is a stronger position than picking a team.
