# Mission: QA Engineering

## Why

Get hired as a QA engineer. The goal is a job offer, not a certificate — which
means the output that matters is demonstrable evidence of testing judgment, plus
the ability to defend that judgment out loud in an interview.

## Success looks like

- Can explain, unprompted, how I decide what to test first when time is short —
  and defend the ranking against pushback.
- Have a public portfolio repo containing a QA strategy, a manual test cycle with
  real evidence, and at least one maintainable end-to-end Playwright suite.
- Can take a vague feature description and produce test cases another person can
  execute without asking me questions.
- Can write a bug report a developer reproduces on the first try.
- Can answer "how would you test this?" in an interview with a structured
  approach rather than a list of guesses.

## Constraints

- 10+ hours per week — compressed relative to the original 27-week sheet.
- Plan of record is the Google Sheet (52 tasks, QA-001 → QA-052).
- Every week should leave a visible artifact in this repo. Employers read the
  repo; they do not read my notes.
- Learning is spread over many months, so retention matters more than
  in-the-moment fluency. Favour spacing and retrieval over re-reading.

## Out of scope

- Sitting the ISTQB exam. The syllabus is the best free primary source and will
  be used heavily as *knowledge* — but passing the exam is not the goal, and
  memorising exam trivia should not displace practice.
- Mobile automation depth (QA-034). Tool-choice literacy is enough unless a
  target role asks for it.
- Building the applications under test. Test against existing open-source or
  sample apps; time spent building is time not spent testing.
