## a. Does the named source cover this?

**No.** Same defect as QA-014: the plan links the *certification*, not the *syllabus*.
Confirmed this session — the URL returns **HTTP 403 Forbidden** to automated fetch, and
even in a browser it is an exam product page (structure, accreditation, booking). There is
no charter, no session sheet, no note-taking guidance. A learner sent here cannot produce
the deliverable because there is nothing on the page to produce it from.

**But do not just swap in the syllabus — it will not carry this deliverable either.**
Fetched and read: ISTQB CTFL v4.0.1 §4.4.2 "Exploratory Testing" (p.44) is **three
paragraphs**, and the learning objective is **FL-4.4.2 (K2) Explain exploratory testing**
(p.38). K2 is *explain*, not *use*. Contrast the black-box objectives on the same page —
FL-4.2.1 to FL-4.2.4 are all "(K3) **Use** ... to derive test cases". ISTQB deliberately
never asks a Foundation candidate to run a session. You can pass the exam having done zero
exploratory testing.

§4.4.2 does name every part the deliverable needs — time box, test charter, debriefing,
test session sheets — but names them in one sentence each and supplies no template, no
example, and no worked session. It is a glossary entry, not a set of instructions. Use it
for vocabulary alignment; get the method from (b).

## b. Best primary source

**Jonathan Bach, "Session-Based Test Management" (STQE, Nov 2000).** Free, stable, 10
pages, and it is the *origin document* for the exact artifact QA-015 asks the learner to
produce. Written by the two people who invented the practice at Hewlett-Packard; it is
what ISTQB §4.4.2 is silently summarising.

Why it fits the deliverable better than anything else: it defines the session as "an
uninterrupted block of reviewable, chartered test effort" (p.1), gives the eight-part
session report structure (p.4), and then prints **a complete filled-in example sheet** for
a product called MapMaker (pp.4–6) — including a Risks list, three BUGS and two ISSUES.
The deliverable's three outputs map onto it one-for-one: *questions* → ISSUES, *risks* →
the Risks list in TEST NOTES, *findings* → BUGS.

Pair it with **Bach, *Exploratory Testing Explained* v1.3** for the thinking behind the
artifact — the definition, the skills, eleven example charters, and a real defect story.

On session length: SBTM p.2 says sessions "last 90 minutes, give or take", with ~45 min
called a *short session* and ~2 hours a *long session*. The plan's 60 minutes sits between
the two. That is legitimate, but the learner should label it a short session rather than
assume 60 is the standard — no source treats 60 min as canonical.

## c. What it leaves out

- **It is a *management* system, and the learner is alone.** Half of SBTM is metrics
  normalisation, a Perl scan tool doing "about 80 syntax and consistency checks" (p.6), a
  sheet repository, and a manager-led debrief. A solo 60-minute session needs none of it.
  Strip the TBS percentages and the tooling; keep charter, notes, issues, bugs, debrief.
- **Neither source tells you how to generate test ideas.** A charter says *where* to look,
  never *what to try*. This is the single biggest gap for a beginner staring at a blank
  session — they will click around aimlessly for 60 minutes. Needs a heuristic set bolted
  on (see (e)).
- **"Risk" is never defined.** SBTM defines bugs and issues crisply (p.4) but risks appear
  only as an undefined ad-hoc list inside the MapMaker notes. Since the deliverable
  explicitly demands risks, the lesson must supply that definition itself.
- **No guidance on what makes a charter good.** Bach prints eleven and then concedes they
  are ambiguous (p.5) — fine for trained staff, useless as a model for a novice.
- **No stopping heuristic beyond the clock**, and no note-taking discipline — which is
  awkward, because Bach's own story (p.9) ends with him losing his notes to an OS freeze.

## d. Credible disagreement

Real and precise — do not blend these.

1. **Is ET a technique, or a property of all testing?** ISTQB §4.4 lists exploratory
   testing as one of three "experience-based test techniques", a peer of error guessing and
   checklist-based testing — something you select. Bach denies it is a technique at all:
   it is a continuum, and "virtually all testing performed by human testers is exploratory
   to *some degree*" (ET Explained p.3). Under his framing, "use exploratory testing" is
   not a menu choice; the right question is *how much* of your testing is exploratory.
2. **Fallback vs. first choice.** ISTQB frames ET as useful "when there are few or
   inadequate specifications or there is significant time pressure" (§4.4.2) — a coping
   strategy. Bach opens by claiming it can be "orders of magnitude more productive than
   scripted testing" (p.1) and lists contexts where it wins outright (p.7).
3. **Explain vs. practise.** ISTQB tests this at K2. Bach closes: "you don't become a black
   belt by reading books. You have to work at it" (p.10). QA-015's deliverable sides with
   Bach, which is the correct call — say so in the lesson.

Worth noting for balance: **RST is self-critical here too.** Jonathan Bach devotes a
section to "Seems like too much bureaucracy" (p.10), concedes SBTM "does impose a structure
that is not strictly necessary", and warns the metrics can be gamed — "A silver-tongued
tester could bias the sheets and manipulate the debriefing in such a way as to fool the
test manager" (p.8). Present SBTM as one accountability option, not doctrine.

## e. Worked examples worth adapting

- **Eleven charters for DecideRight** (ET Explained p.5) — ready-made charter examples at
  varying quality. Bach's own admission that they are ambiguous makes a good "rewrite these
  to be sharper" exercise.
- **"ET in Action"** (ET Explained pp.8–9) — the best single artefact here. Bach has a
  4-hour mission to check a photo editor against Windows Compatibility standards. He tours
  the menus, spots a memory-limit dialog, sets memory to 5%, makes a 100-inch canvas, fills
  it with purple dots, applies a ripple effect → "Error -32: Sorry this Error is Fatal" →
  then pushes memory the other way and **freezes all of Windows 2000**, disqualifying the
  product ~30 minutes in. Shows exactly how one result seeds the next test. Bonus: he loses
  his notes to the freeze — a free argument for durable note-taking.
- **The MapMaker session sheet** (SBTM pp.4–6) — a complete worked deliverable: CHARTER,
  #AREAS, TBS 65/25/20, charter-vs-opportunity 100/0, a coverage outline, five risks, three
  bugs, two issues. Hand this to the learner as the target artifact.
- **"Notes From the Field"** (SBTM pp.8–9) — eight real failure modes as Q&A (can't fulfil
  the charter, whole session spent off-charter, interruptions, two testers on one session).
  Ideal for a "when it goes sideways" section.
- **PROOF debrief** (SBTM pp.9–10) — Past / Results / Obstacles / Outlook / Feelings.
  Adapt to a solo self-debrief to close the 60 minutes.
- **Test Heuristics Cheat Sheet** (Hendrickson, Lyndsay, Emery) — ~20 named heuristics
  (Boundaries, Goldilocks, CRUD, Starvation, Flood, Interruptions, Multi-user…). Fills the
  idea-generation gap in (c). Caveat: the canonical testobsessed.com URL fails TLS
  verification as of this session; only mirrors resolve. Verify a host before linking it.

  better stated as 'In what ways and to what degree is your testing exploratory?'"

  problems that relate to the test process or the project at large."

  order to achieve the mission of good testing."
