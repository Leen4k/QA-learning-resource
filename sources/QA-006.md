## c. What it leaves out

1. **What to leave out** — the learner's actual stated want. §5.1 is purely additive: seven content categories, zero words on length, zero on what to cut, zero on when a plan isn't worth writing. "One-page" never appears.
2. **Any template or worked plan.** §5.1.1 ends by deferring to ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-3, which is not free (iso.org blocked my fetch — verify before citing a price). The free source hands you a shopping list and points at a paywall for the artifact itself.
3. **The permissions domain.** Nothing on roles × resources × actions, no permission matrix, no decision-table modelling of an access rule.
4. **Who reads it, who signs it, when it's revised.** §5.1.1 says a plan "serves as a means of communication" but never with whom, at what cadence, or what a reader is supposed to *do* with it.
5. **Scale mismatch.** §5.1.4 estimation (ratios, extrapolation, Wideband Delphi, three-point) is project-scale machinery. A one-page feature plan needs one line of effort, not a Delphi round.

## d. Credible disagreement

**Rapid Software Testing (Bach & Bolton) vs ISTQB — the disagreement is over what the word "plan" denotes.**

ISTQB treats the test plan as a *document with required sections* and points to ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-3 for the template. Bach defines a test plan as **"the set of ideas that guide the test process"** — explicitly not a document; he argues the dictionary sense of "plan" requires no documentation at all (https://www.satisfice.us/articles/et_myth.shtml). Under RST the artifact is a by-product; the thinking is the plan.

Not an abstract quibble: the standard ISTQB cites in §5.1.1 is the one the context-driven community petitioned to withdraw (#stop29119, 2014; petition by Iain McCowatt of the ISST, Bach a charter member). Bolton on that standard: *"A moment that a tester spends on useless documentation is a moment in which she's not focused on identifying risks and finding problems that would cause loss, harm, or annoyance"* — and its *"focus on the artifacts and the process model displace and de-centre the most important part of any testing effort: the skill set and the mindset of the individual tester."*

Don't blend these. Honest lesson framing: ISTQB tells you the seven things a reader might need; RST tells you the page is only worth writing if it changes what someone does. A one-page plan is where the two conflict least — brevity forces the ISTQB list through an RST filter. Say that, rather than presenting ISTQB's list as the answer.

## e. Worked examples worth adapting

- **OWASP Top 10 A01:2021 Broken Access Control** — https://owasp.org/Top10/2021/A01_2021-Broken_Access_Control/index.html — the risk register for this exact deliverable, pre-written and authoritative. 34 CWEs mapped, 19,013 CVEs, 3.81% avg incidence — the #1 category. Its weakness list (IDOR, force browsing to admin pages, parameter/URL tampering, missing POST/PUT/DELETE controls on APIs, privilege escalation, JWT manipulation, CORS misconfig) converts one-for-one into risk rows. Attack Scenario #1 (`?acct=notmyacct`) is a 30-second real defect story.
- **Heuristic Test Strategy Model v6.0, Bach** — https://www.developsense.com/resource/htsm.pdf (read this session, pp.1–2). Nine technique families: Function, Domain, Stress, Flow, Scenario, Claims, User, Risk, Tool-Supported. **User Testing** ("Identify categories and roles of users") and **Risk Testing** ("What kinds of problems could the product have? Which kinds matter most?") write the test-approach line of a permissions plan. Use as the counter-model to §5.1.1.
- **ISTQB §5.1.4 three-point estimation, worked in the syllabus:** a=6, m=9, b=18 → E = (6 + 4×9 + 18)/6 = 10, SD = (18−6)/6 = 2, so 10±2 person-hours. Small enough to be the effort line without swamping the page. Its ratio example (dev:test 3:2, 600 dev person-days → 400 test person-days) is a good foil for why ratio estimation misfires at feature scale.
- **Bach's Test Plan Evaluation Model** — https://www.satisfice.com/download/test-plan-evaluation-model ("I developed this model to provide a basis to systematically assess the quality of test plans", 1999, rev. 2021). A grading rubric for the learner's deliverable. **I could not open the PDF this session** (direct links 404) — retrieve it before relying on it.

## Verbatim quotes

**§5.1.1, p.47** — "A test plan describes the objectives, resources and processes for a test project. A test plan: • Documents the means and schedule for achieving test objectives • Helps to ensure that the performed test activities will meet the established criteria • Serves as a means of communication with team members and other stakeholders • Demonstrates that testing will adhere to the existing test policy and test strategy (or explains why the testing will deviate from them)"

**§5.1.1, p.47** — "Test planning guides the testers' thinking and forces the testers to confront the future challenges related to risks, schedules, people, tools, costs, effort, etc." ... "More details about the test plan and its content can be found in the ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-3 standard."

**§5.1.1, p.47** — "The typical content of a test plan includes: • Context of testing (e.g., scope, test objectives, constraints, test basis) • Assumptions and constraints of the test project • Stakeholders (e.g., roles, responsibilities, relevance to testing, hiring and training needs) • Communication (e.g., forms and frequency of communication, documentation templates) • Risk register (e.g., product risks, project risks) • Test approach (e.g., test levels, test types, test techniques, test deliverables, entry criteria and exit criteria, independence of testing, metrics to be collected, test data requirements, test environment requirements, deviations from the organizational test policy and test strategy) • Budget and schedule"

**§5.1.3, p.48** — "Entry criteria define the preconditions for undertaking a given activity... Exit criteria define what must be achieved in order to declare an activity completed." ... "Running out of time or budget can also be viewed as valid exit criteria... if the stakeholders have reviewed and accepted the risk to go live without further testing." ... "In Agile software development, exit criteria are often called Definition of Done... Entry criteria that a user story must fulfill to start the development and/or testing activities are called Definition of Ready."

**§5.2.3, p.51** — "the goal of product risk analysis is to provide an awareness of product risk in order to focus the testing effort in a way that minimizes the residual level of product risk."

**HTSM v6.0, p.1** — "The Heuristic Test Strategy Model is a set of patterns for designing and choosing tests to perform. The immediate purpose of this model is to remind testers of what to think about during that process. I encourage testers to customize it to fit their own organizations and contexts."

**OWASP A01:2021** — "Access control enforces policy such that users cannot act outside of their intended permissions. Failures typically lead to unauthorized information disclosure, modification, or destruction of all data or performing a business function outside the user's limits."
