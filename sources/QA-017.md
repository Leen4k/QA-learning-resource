## c. What it leaves out

The gap is sharp, and it is the whole assignment:

- **CTFL teaches prioritization, not selection.** §5.1.5 orders a suite; it never draws a cut
  line, and the DELIVERABLE requires deciding what *doesn't* run. Check the objectives:
  regression is **FL-2.2.3 (K2) "Distinguish confirmation testing from regression testing"** —
  recall level. There is no K3 objective anywhere in the syllabus on building or scoping a
  regression pack. Foundation does not ask you to do this.
- **No method for impact analysis.** §2.2.3 calls it "advisable" and says it "shows which parts
  of the software could be affected" — never how. No dependency mapping, no worked case.
- **No pack sizing and no retirement rule.** Nothing on a time budget, a target size, or what
  to do when the pack outgrows the release window; §2.2.3 only observes that it *grows*. P5
  says tests wear out and "may need to be modified" but never says when to delete one. A pack
  that only ever grows is the real industry failure mode.
- **No worked risk matrix, and no calibration.** §5.2.3 names the qualitative matrix in one
  clause and shows no example — the learner invents the axes and the scale. Nothing on where
  likelihood estimates come from, or that multiplying two ordinal scores is not arithmetic.

## d. Credible disagreement

**Named, not blended: heuristics versus arithmetic.**

ISTQB §5.2.3 offers to compute risk — "the risk level is calculated as the multiplication of
risk likelihood and risk impact." Bach's **Heuristic Test Strategy Model v6.0** (verbatim,
fetched) refuses that framing throughout: it is "a set of patterns for designing and choosing
tests… to remind testers of what to think about"; its Risk Testing entry is four questions,
not a formula; Quality Criteria are "categories of product risk" that are "subjective and
multidimensional and often hidden or contradictory" — the opposite of a number you multiply.
Its regression-relevant prompts are questions about change: "New Stuff: Do you know what has
recently been changed or added in the product?"

Practical bite: ISTQB's matrix makes the pack look objective and hides who guessed the
numbers; HTSM makes the guess explicit but gives no defensible cut line. A good lesson makes
the learner produce the matrix *and* record who supplied each score. If you want the tension
without leaving ISTQB, §1.3 principle 5 already supplies it: a rerun-everything pack is, by
the syllabus's own principle, decreasingly effective at finding new defects.

**Two further RST claims I could not verify — do not quote until fetched.** (i) A third-party
rendering of Bach's *Heuristic Risk-Based Testing* (1999) gives "I've never been on a project
where we felt the cost of rigorous analysis was justified," the inside-out/outside-in
distinction, and generic-risk guidewords (Complex, New, Changed, Upstream/Downstream
Dependency, Critical, Popular, Third-party, Buggy, Recent failure) — near-ideal here, but it
reached me as **summary, not verbatim**; get the real PDF from satisfice.com first. (ii) A
search snippet attributes to Bach the view that regression bugs are not especially severe,
making the regression/new axis the wrong one. **Unfetched.**

## e. Worked examples worth adapting

- **Ariane 5 Flight 501 Inquiry Board report (July 1996)** —
  https://esamultimedia.esa.int/docs/esa-x-1819eng.pdf (scanned; readable, quotes below). The
  best regression case study I found, because the failed component was **unchanged** — the
  *environment* changed. Reused Ariane 4 alignment software, a function that "serves no
  purpose" after lift-off, destroyed the launcher 37 seconds in because Ariane 5's trajectory
  produced larger horizontal-velocity values. Maps exactly onto CTFL §2.3's environment-
  migration trigger. Classroom use: give the change description *before* the outcome and ask
  what goes in the pack — nearly everyone scopes to changed code and misses it.
- **Facebook, "Predictive Test Selection"** (Machalica, Samylkin, Porth, Chandra;
  arXiv:1810.05286) — https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.05286. The industrial answer to "instead of
  rerunning everything," with numbers: cost halved while still catching ">95% of individual
  test failures and over 99.9% of faulty changes." Use it to force the question the deliverable
  turns on — *what recall are you willing to accept?* A risk-based pack is a bet on missing
  something; this is the only source here that sizes the bet.
- **Exercise from principle 5.** Hand the learner a bloated pack and make them cut it to a
  fixed time budget, justifying each deletion against a risk score. Cutting teaches the
  criterion better than writing from blank — the same move that worked in QA-016.
- **Not obtained:** the SEC's Knight Capital order (sec.gov 403s this tool) and Google's
  "Taming Google-Scale Continuous Testing" (Memon et al., ICSE-SEIP 2017) — strong candidates,
  unread this session, so not quotable yet.

## Verbatim quotes

**CTFL v4.0.1 §1.3 Testing Principles (p.18):**
- "**2. Exhaustive testing is impossible**. Testing everything is not feasible except in trivial cases (Manna 1978). Rather than attempting to test exhaustively, test techniques (see chapter 4), test case prioritization (see section 5.1.5), and risk-based testing (see section 5.2), should be used to focus test efforts."
- "**4. Defects cluster together**. A small number of system components usually contain most of the defects discovered or are responsible for most of the operational failures (Enders 1975). … Predicted defect clusters, and actual defect clusters observed during testing or in operation, are an important input for risk-based testing."
- "**5. Tests wear out**. If the same tests are repeated many times, they become increasingly ineffective in detecting new defects (Beizer 1990). … However, in some cases, repeating the same tests can have a beneficial outcome, e.g., in automated regression testing."

**CTFL v4.0.1 §2.2.3 (pp.30–31):**
- "**Regression testing** confirms that no adverse consequences have been caused by a change, including a fix that has already been confirmation tested. These adverse consequences could affect the same component where the change was made, other components in the same system, or even other connected systems."
- "It is advisable first to perform an impact analysis to recognize the extent of the regression testing. Impact analysis shows which parts of the software could be affected."
- "Regression test suites are run many times and generally the number of regression test cases will increase with each iteration or release, so regression testing is a strong candidate for automation."

**CTFL v4.0.1 §2.3 (p.31), §5.1.5 (p.50), §5.2.3 (pp.52–53):**
- "The scope of maintenance testing typically depends on: The degree of risk of the change / The size of the existing system / The size of the change"
- "Upgrades or migrations of the operational environment, such as from one platform to another, which can require tests associated with the new environment as well as of the changed software…"
- "Risk-based prioritization, where the order of test execution is based on the results of risk analysis (see section 5.2.3). Test cases covering the most important risks are executed first."
- "Risk assessment can use a quantitative or qualitative approach, or a mix of them. In the quantitative approach the risk level is calculated as the multiplication of risk likelihood and risk impact. In the qualitative approach the risk level can be determined using a risk matrix."

**CTFL v4.0.1 Learning Objectives (p.24, p.47) — evidence of the gap:**
- "FL-2.2.3 (K2) Distinguish confirmation testing from regression testing" / "FL-2.3.1 (K2) Summarize maintenance testing and its triggers" / "FL-5.1.5 (K3) Apply test case prioritization" ← the only K3 anywhere near this task

**Bach, Heuristic Test Strategy Model v6.0, 2/8/2024 (developsense.com/resource/htsm.pdf):**
- "**Quality Criteria Categories** are dimensions in which people determine the value of the product. You can also think of them as categories of product risk. Quality criteria are subjective and multidimensional and often hidden or contradictory." (p.1)
- "**Risk Testing** — *Imagine a problem, then look for it.* 1. What kinds of problems could the product have? 2. Which kinds matter most? Focus on those. 3. How would you detect them if they were there?" (p.2)
- "*New Stuff:* Do you know what has recently been changed or added in the product?" / "*Volatility:* Is the product constantly changing? How will you find out about changes?" (p.3)

**Ariane 501 Inquiry Board report (July 1996), §2.1–2.3:**
- "The error occurred in a part of the software that only performs alignment of the strap-down inertial platform. This software module computes meaningful results only before lift-off. As soon as the launcher lifts off, this function serves no purpose." (p.4)
- "The value of BH was much higher than expected because the early part of the trajectory of Ariane 5 differs from that of Ariane 4 and results in considerably higher horizontal velocity values." (p.4)
- "…it was maintained for commonality reasons, presumably based on the view that, unless proven necessary, it was not wise to make changes in software which worked well on Ariane 4." (p.6)
- "Testing at equipment level was in the case of the SRI conducted rigorously with regard to all environmental factors and in fact beyond what was expected for Ariane 5. However, no test was performed to verify that the SRI would behave correctly when being subjected to the count-down and flight time sequence and the trajectory of Ariane 5." (p.7)
- "Had such a test been performed by the supplier or as part of the acceptance test, the failure mechanism would have been exposed." (p.7)
- "…there was a definite risk in assuming that critical equipment such as the SRI had been validated by qualification on its own, or by previous use on Ariane 4." (p.9)

**Facebook, "Predictive Test Selection" (arXiv:1810.05286), abstract:**
- "a large number of tests coupled with a high rate of changes committed to our monolithic repository make it infeasible to run all potentially-impacted tests on each change."
- "Deployed in production, the strategy reduces the total infrastructure cost of testing code changes by a factor of two, while guaranteeing that over 95% of individual test failures and over 99.9% of faulty changes are still reported back to developers."
