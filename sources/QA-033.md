# QA-033 — Mobile test strategy

> Dossier, not lesson content. Every claim below must be re-read at source
> before it ships in a lesson.

- **Verdict:** WRONG-DOCUMENT
- **Named source:** https://appium.io/docs/en/latest/
- **Best primary source:** ISTQB *Certified Tester Specialist — Mobile Application Testing (CT-MAT), Foundation Level*, Version 2019 (released 3 May 2019, 51 pp.) — landing page https://istqb.org/certifications/certified-tester-mobile-application-testing-ct-mat/ (403s to fetchers); readable PDF mirror https://anztb.org/wp-content/uploads/ISTQB-MAT-syllabus.pdf
- **Exact sections to read:** CT-MAT §1.1 (pp.11–12) · §1.6–1.8 (pp.16–18) · §2.1.8 (p.23) · §2.2.5–2.3 (pp.25–26) · §4.3–4.4 (pp.37–38) — 11 pages. Then Android *Core app quality* → "Test environment" and "Privacy and Security". Then HTSM v6.0 pp.1 and 4.
- **Read time:** ~55 min

## a. Does the named source cover this?

No. The Appium docs teach a driver-based automation framework, not a strategy.
I fetched the site; the entire nav is Introduction (How Appium Works, Drivers,
Clients, History) · Quickstart (System Requirements, Installation, Writing Tests)
· Ecosystem (Drivers, Clients, Plugins, Tools) · Reference (CLI, Session
Properties, API Endpoints) · Guides (Migration, Server/Driver Configuration,
Session Configuration) · Developing · Contributing.

There is no page on device coverage, OS-version selection, risk analysis,
permissions testing, or network conditions. The nearest thing is "Session
Configuration," which is capability syntax — how to *address* a device you have
already decided to test, which is the question this task is about. A learner who
reads all of it can drive a phone from code and still has no risk list and no
matrix. Appium belongs in the *execution* task, not this one.

## b. Best primary source

**CT-MAT is a direct hit — this task is essentially chapter 1 of it.** Unusually,
the syllabus is deliverable-shaped:

- **§1.6 "Test Strategy for Mobile Apps"** (p.16) names the three coverage
  postures and forces a choice: single-platform, multi-platform, maximum
  coverage. That is the spine of the matrix.
- **§1.8 "Risks in Mobile Application Testing"** (p.18) is a five-row
  risk/mitigation *table* — the risk-list deliverable, ready to extend. §1.7
  (pp.17–18) supplies ~12 more candidate risks to populate it.
- **§4.4 "Setting up a Test Lab"** (p.38) gives the matrix *columns* explicitly:
  "rank of the device (as found in Google or other analytics), operating system
  and versions, screen dimensions and density, availability and cost, special
  features, and importance in targeted audience."
- The four axes in the task's `wants` line map one-to-one: devices §1.1/§4.4,
  OS versions §2.2.5, permissions §2.1.8, networks §2.3.
- **HO-1.7.1** (p.11) is the deliverable, pre-written as an exercise (see §e).

Second source, for the concreteness ISTQB refuses to give: **Android *Core app
quality*** — https://developer.android.com/docs/quality-guidelines/core-app-quality.
Named emulator form factors, five testable permission criteria, and numbered
test procedures with IDs (`T-Orientation_Transitions`, `T-Audio_Playback_Background`).

Framing counterweight: **Android *Device compatibility overview*** —
https://developer.android.com/guide/practices/compatibility.

## c. What it leaves out

- **It is seven years old, and mobile moved.** §2.1.8 talks about permissions
  "denied at installation" — that is the pre-Android-6 install-time model.
  Nothing on runtime prompts, one-time grants, permission auto-reset, scoped
  storage, background-location restrictions, or App Tracking Transparency.
  §2.3's "2G, 3G, 4G and 5G" list includes networks since switched off in many
  markets. **The lesson must correct this, not relay it.**
- **No worked matrix and no arithmetic.** §4.4 lists the columns; no filled
  example exists anywhere in 51 pages. HO-1.7.1 asks learners to "calculate the
  expected market coverage" for five devices — and the syllabus never once shows
  that calculation.
- **No stopping rule.** Nothing on how many rows is enough, what budget buys
  what coverage, or which subset runs per-commit vs. per-release. It says
  maximum coverage "is usually not economically viable" and leaves the cut line
  to the reader.
- **No foldables, large screens, or wearables** as coverage axes.
- **No defect stories.** Zero worked failures. Both illustrative examples in
  §1.7 are two sentences of hypothetical.
- **Data sources are stale**: cites StatCounter GlobalStats and little else.

## d. Credible disagreement

**RST rejects the matrix as the unit of strategy.** Bach's *Heuristic Test
Strategy Model* v6.0 (8 Feb 2024, satisfice.com; readable copy at
https://www.developsense.com/resource/htsm.pdf) derives strategy from Project
Environment + Product Elements + Quality Criteria, and **Platform is one of
eight Product Elements** — sharing the page with Structure, Function, Data,
Interfaces, Operations, Time. CT-MAT makes platform coverage nearly the whole
strategy. HTSM's output is not a grid at all: it is "The Testing and Quality
Story," and Bach says outright you "can't 'verify' quality, as such." A filled
device matrix with green ticks is exactly the artifact HTSM declines to produce.
Note HTSM also invites customisation — "I encourage testers to customize it" —
so this is a difference of emphasis with real teeth, not a doctrinal war.
Worth noting: CT-MAT §3.3 itself imports the RST toolkit (tours, SBTM,
mnemonics), so the syllabus is arguing with itself about which one is primary.

**Google disagrees with ISTQB about which OS versions to test.** CT-MAT §1.6
says reduce to what "a majority of customers" use — which skews *old*. Android
*Core app quality* says: "you should always test against the latest version of
Android to ensure the latest behavior changes don't negatively impact your app."
Popularity finds the users; recency finds the breakage. A matrix built only on
market share systematically omits the OS that will break you next quarter.

**Google also disputes the row unit.** The *Device compatibility overview* says
device compatibility is settled by CTS before a device ships Play Store, so the
developer's problem is *configuration* compatibility — "support as many device
configurations as possible using a single APK or AAB." That argues the matrix
rows should be configurations (density, screen size, feature availability, API
level), not device model names. CT-MAT's §4.4 columns mix both.

## e. Worked examples worth adapting

- **HO-1.7.1 (p.11) is the deliverable verbatim** — hand it out unchanged:
  "Gather market data such as device or operating system market share for a
  selected region. Gather data for screen sizes and density. Create a list of
  five devices and calculate the expected market coverage for this list."
  The "five" and the "calculate" are what make it a real exercise. Pair with
  **HO-1.1.1**, which adds prioritization.
- **HO-2.1.8 (p.19)** is a ready permissions lab: "Test an app's permissions
  management by permitting and denying requested permissions and observing
  behavior when folders and sensor settings are denied at installation or
  changed after installation." Modernise "at installation" per §c.
- **§1.8's risk table (p.18)** — five rows, Risk / Possible mitigation. Give
  learners the left column and have them write the right, then compare.
- **Two risk-generating mnemonics, both printed in the syllabus** (§3.3.1,
  p.32): **I SLICED UP FUN** (Kohl) and **SFDiPOT** (from HTSM). Use one as the
  checklist that turns "mobile is risky" into an enumerated list.
  ⚠ kohl.ca was **unreachable** (ECONNREFUSED) this session — I could not read
  Kohl's original article. Cite the syllabus's rendering, not the source essay.
- **§3.3.3 tour tables (p.33)** — two ready-made tables (Landmark-tour analogies;
  ~17 named tours incl. Connectivity, Low battery, Light, Orientation, Motion).
  Directly usable as the exploratory half of a mobile strategy.
- **Android's `T-` test procedures** are the concrete steps CT-MAT never gives —
  e.g. `T-Orientation_Transitions`: "From each app screen, rotate the device
  between landscape and portrait orientations and folded and unfolded states at
  least three times."
- **Gap: no real defect story found.** Neither source contains a citable
  postmortem. Build one from §2.2.5's Doze example (an OS-version behaviour
  change breaking an app on some versions and not others) and label it as
  constructed.

## Verbatim quotes

**[PDF]** = read off the page image, reliable. **[FETCH]** = via a summarizing
fetch — **re-read at source before shipping.**

**[PDF] CT-MAT §1.6 (pp.16–17), the three postures:** "Single platform approach: Reduce scope to a single type of device, one OS version, one carrier and one network type." · "Multi-platform approach: Reduce scope to a representative selection of devices and OS used by a majority of customers in the target market, based on mobile traffic or other analytical data." · "Maximum coverage approach: Cover all OS versions, devices, manufacturers, carriers and network types. This is basically exhaustive testing, which is usually not economically viable, especially when considering the multitude of devices and OS versions on the market."

**[PDF] CT-MAT §1.6 (p.16), risks:** "Without knowing the device proliferation data in a particular geographic location, one cannot choose the devices on which the app needs to be tested in a sustainable fashion." · challenges list incl. "The variety of mobile devices with device-specific defects on some of them"; "The availability of devices in-house or via the use of external test labs"; "Network coverage and its impact on the app in a global context."

**[PDF] CT-MAT §1.8 (p.18), risk/mitigation table:** "Market fragmentation → Choose an appropriate selection of devices for test execution, e.g., testing the most commonly used devices." · "Cost of supporting multiple platforms → Perform analysis to understand most used platforms, thus avoiding testing of those no longer in scope." · "Lack of availability of devices for test execution → Apply remote device access services or crowd testing services."

**[PDF] CT-MAT §2.1.8 (p.23):** "The app is able to work with reduced permissions; it asks the user to grant these permissions and does not fail in an unexplained manner." · "Permissions are only requested for the resources which are relevant to the app's functionality; no broad permissions for unrelated resources are allowed."

**[PDF] CT-MAT §2.3 (pp.25–26):** "In real-world usage, connectivity methods differ. Users can be continuously connected using one particular mode, or they can switch between modes… While on the move they may even hit dead spots with no network at all." · "Switching between modes does not cause any unexpected behavior or data loss."

**[PDF] CT-MAT §4.4 (p.38), the matrix columns:** "Device selection can be done on the basis of various factors such as rank of the device (as found in Google or other analytics), operating system and versions, screen dimensions and density, availability and cost, special features, and importance in targeted audience."

**[PDF] HTSM v6.0 (2024-02-08), p.4, Platform:** "Everything on which the product depends (and that is outside your project)." · p.1: "Product Elements are aspects of the product that you consider testing… Take care to cover all of it that matters, not just the parts that are easy to see." · "You can never know the 'actual' quality of a software product— you can't 'verify' quality, as such."

**[FETCH] Android *Core app quality*, Test environment:** "You don't need to test on every device that's on the market. Focus on a small number of representative devices, even using one or two devices per form factor." · "Test with the latest Android version: In addition to testing representative Android versions for your target user base, you should always test against the latest version of Android to ensure the latest behavior changes don't negatively impact your app's user experience."

**[FETCH] Android *Core app quality*, permissions:** "App requests only the absolute minimum permissions needed to support the current use case." · "App requests runtime permissions when the functionality is requested, rather than during app startup." · "App gracefully degrades when users deny or revoke a permission. App shouldn't prevent user access altogether."

**[FETCH] Android *Device compatibility overview*:** "To achieve the largest user base possible for your app, support as many device configurations as possible using a single APK or AAB."
