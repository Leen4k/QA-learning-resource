# QA-011 — Agile ways of working: what QA actually does inside a sprint

> Dossier, not lesson content. Every claim below must be re-read at source
> before it ships in a lesson.

- **Verdict:** PARTIAL
- **Named source:** https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html
- **Best primary source:** ISTQB CTFL Extension Syllabus — Agile Tester, Version 2014 (30 Sep 2014, 43pp) — canonical https://istqb.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/ISTQB-CTFL-AT_Syllabus_v1.0.pdf (403s to bots); the mirror I read this session is https://astqb.org/assets/documents/ISTQB-Foundation-Agile-Syllabus-.pdf
- **Exact sections to read:** CTFL-AT §2.3.2 (p.26), §1.2.5 (pp.16–17), §2.1.3 (p.21), §2.2.1–2.2.2 (pp.23–25), §3.1.4 (pp.30–31), §1.2.1 (pp.11–13), §1.2.3 (p.14), §3.2.1 (pp.31–32) — plus the whole Scrum Guide (13pp) for current vocabulary
- **Read time:** ~75 min (~60 syllabus sections, ~15 Scrum Guide)

## a. Does the named source cover this?

Half of it — and not the half the deliverable needs. The Scrum Guide **does** give
the sprint container: the four events, the artifacts, the timeboxes, the Definition
of Done. That matters — the deliverable is organised around a sprint calendar, and
this is the correct, current authority for Scrum vocabulary.

But it says nothing about QA. I fetched it twice this session with
differently-phrased extraction prompts; both returned **zero occurrences of
"test", "testing", "tester", "QA", or "quality assurance"** in the whole document.
"Quality" appears only in the abstract — "Instilling quality by adhering to a
Definition of Done", and "plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness"
(Retrospective). Neither tells a learner what to *do*. It also cannot address the
second half of the goal — "how that changes across team methods" — because it
describes exactly one method. No Kanban, no XP, no comparison.

The ISTQB Agile Tester syllabus makes the criticism for me, §1.2.1 (p.12): Scrum
"does not provide guidance on how testing has to be done in a Scrum project." A
learner sent only here can name the four events and still not list a single QA
task. Keep it as the skeleton the real source fills in — roughly 20% of this task.

## b. Best primary source

**ISTQB CTFL Agile Tester extension syllabus, Version 2014** — free, stable,
standards-body, and §2.3.2 is very nearly the deliverable already:

- **§2.3.2 The Role of a Tester in an Agile Team** (p.26) — nine-bullet list of
  in-sprint QA activities. The spine of the deliverable.
- **§1.2.5 Release and Iteration Planning** (pp.16–17) — two explicit lists of
  what testers add value to, release vs iteration. Objective FA-1.2.5 is literally
  "how a tester adds value in each of these activities".
- **§2.1.3 Test Levels** (p.21) — the *sequence* a story moves through in an
  iteration: unit → feature verification → feature validation, plus parallel
  regression. Closest thing to a day-shape.
- **§2.2.1–2.2.2** (pp.23–25) — task board, burndown and stand-up questions; then
  the per-iteration chore of reviewing, promoting and retiring regression tests.
- **§3.1.4 The Role of a Tester** (pp.30–31) — Scrum-specific: teamwork attributes,
  Sprint Zero checklist, test planning, pairing, mind-mapping.
- **§1.2.1 Agile Software Development Approaches** (pp.11–13) — XP, Scrum and Kanban
  side by side: the "across team methods" half. Also **§1.2.3 Retrospectives** (p.14)
  and **§3.2.1 Quality Risks** (pp.31–32).

## c. What it leaves out

1. **It is out of date on Scrum itself.** §1.2.1 (p.12) says "Scrum defines three
   roles: Scrum Master... Product Owner... Development Team". The 2020 Scrum Guide
   abolished that framing — one Scrum Team, three *accountabilities*, no
   "Development Team". Read the syllabus for the testing, the Scrum Guide for the
   nouns; the lesson must reconcile them explicitly.
2. **No day-by-day shape.** Every list is per-event, never laid on a calendar. The
   syllabus says sprints are "usually two to four weeks" (§1.2.1) and never addresses
   the two-week squeeze — the story landing on day 9, the last-day crunch,
   carry-over. Neither source supplies this; it is the lesson's own synthesis work.
3. **No effort split**, so the learner cannot sanity-check their list for realism;
   and **Kanban gets one sentence of consequence** — "Iterations or sprints are
   optional in Kanban" (§1.2.1, p.13) — thin for a flow-vs-sprint contrast.
4. **2014 vintage on tooling.** CI is covered (§1.2.4); trunk-based development,
   containerised environments and modern CD are absent. CTFL v4.0.1 §2.1.4–2.1.5
   (DevOps, shift-left) is the likely follow-up — **not** read this session, so
   verify before citing.

## d. Credible disagreement

**Rapid Software Testing (Bolton & Bach) disagrees with the framing, not the task
list.** Read *Rapid Software Testing in Agile Contexts* (Bolton & Bach, Sept 2017) —
https://www.developsense.com/presentations/2017-09-TestingIsTestingAgileIsContext.pdf

1. **Agile does not change what testing is.** The thesis slide is "Testing is
   *testing*. Agile is *context*." (slide 2, crediting Scott Barber). RST rejects
   the premise that Agile testing needs its own syllabus and certification.
2. **Confirmation is not testing.** The syllabus builds the sprint around
   confirming acceptance criteria — §1.2.2 has them "used to confirm that the story
   is done", quadrant Q2 "confirms product behavior" (§3.1.3). Bolton, slide 2
   notes: "Testing might include occasional bursts of confirming things that we
   know, but serious testing involves *challenging* what we know."
3. **A green task board is not evidence.** §2.2.1 has test tasks moving to "done"
   as scripts pass. RST Premise #4 (slide 10): "A test is a performance, not an
   artifact. Testing is not test cases." — "Until some tester engages with the
   product, observes it and interprets those observations, *no testing has
   occurred*." This cuts against §2.2.2's "automate as many tests as possible".

The underlying distinction is in Bach & Bolton's *Testing and Checking Refined*,
https://www.satisfice.com/blog/archives/856 (live page carries an update note
dated 2024-08-10; the much-cited original was titled "…Refactored"): checking is
"the mechanistic process of verifying propositions about the product"; testing is
"evaluating a product by learning about it through experiencing, exploring, and
experimenting".

Where they **agree**: §2.3.2's own risk list warns that "Testers work so closely to
developers that they lose the appropriate tester mindset" and that testers "become
tolerant of or silent about... low-quality practices" — an RST argument sitting
inside the ISTQB document, and good lesson material.

## e. Worked examples worth adapting

- **§3.2.1 (p.32) — six-step quality-risk process run during iteration planning**,
  from "Gather the Agile team members together, including the tester(s)" to "Select
  the appropriate test technique(s) to mitigate each risk". Adapt as the
  sprint-planning exercise. Same page gives **three concrete quality risks** with
  characteristics named: incorrect calculations in reports (accuracy), slow response
  to user input (efficiency), unclear screens and fields (usability).
- **§3.1.4 (p.30) — Sprint Zero checklist**, nine setup activities including
  "Specify the definition of 'done'" and "Create the task board" — the
  "before sprint 1" contrast to recurring sprint work.
- **§2.3.2 (p.26) — nine-bullet role list** as a card sort: which happen every
  sprint, which once per release? Its three named organizational risks work as
  failure modes to discuss.
- **§3.1.3 (p.29) — four testing quadrants**, concrete test types per quadrant:
  "which of these fit in a two-week sprint at all?"

**No narrated defect stories in either source** — both stay at the level of activity
lists, so a real defect story must come from elsewhere or the learner's own work.

## Verbatim quotes

- **Scrum Guide, Developers:** "Developers are always accountable for: Creating a
  plan for the Sprint, the Sprint Backlog; Instilling quality by adhering to a
  Definition of Done; Adapting their plan each day toward the Sprint Goal; and,
  Holding each other accountable as professionals."
- **Scrum Guide, Increment:** "Work cannot be considered part of an Increment
  unless it meets the Definition of Done. If a Product Backlog item does not meet
  the Definition of Done, it cannot be released or even presented at the Sprint
  Review."
- **Scrum Guide, Sprint:** "They are fixed length events of one month or less to
  create consistency." (Two weeks is conformant but never described.)
- **CTFL-AT §1.2.1 (p.12):** "Scrum (as opposed to XP) does not dictate specific
  software development techniques (e.g., test first programming). In addition,
  Scrum does not provide guidance on how testing has to be done in a Scrum
  project."
- **CTFL-AT §2.3.2 (p.26), first 3 of 9:** "Understanding, implementing, and
  updating the test strategy / Measuring and reporting test coverage across all
  applicable coverage dimensions / Ensuring proper use of testing tools..." — and
  "each team member is responsible for product quality and plays a role in
  performing test-related tasks."
- **CTFL-AT §2.1.3 (p.21):** "During an iteration, any given user story will
  typically progress sequentially through the following test activities: Unit
  testing, typically done by the developer / Feature acceptance testing... Feature
  verification testing... Feature validation testing..."
