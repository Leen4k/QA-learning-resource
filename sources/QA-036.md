# QA-036 — Lighthouse and web performance: reading a score you cannot trust

> Dossier, not lesson content. Every claim below must be re-read at source
> before it ships in a lesson.

- **Verdict:** PARTIAL
- **Named source:** https://developer.chrome.com/docs/devtools
- **Best primary source:** Lighthouse, *"Score Variability"* — https://github.com/GoogleChrome/lighthouse/blob/main/docs/variability.md
- **Exact sections to read:** variability.md §*Summary* → §*Sources of Variability* (all 7) → §*Effect of Throttling Strategies* (table) → §*Strategies for Dealing With Variance* · Chrome *Lighthouse performance scoring* §*How scores are color-coded* + §*Why your score fluctuates* · Chrome *Lighthouse* (DevTools) §*Lighthouse is influenced by your setup* · web.dev *Lab and field data differences*
- **Read time:** ~35 min

## a. Does the named source cover this?

Not as given. `developer.chrome.com/docs/devtools` is the DevTools **section index**
— a nav tree (AI assistance · Panels · Settings · Accessibility · For agents) with
promo blurbs, where Lighthouse appears only as a link in the panel list. No prose on
scoring, none on variability, nothing a learner can act on. Zero of the 4h belongs
on this URL.

PARTIAL, not WRONG-DOCUMENT, for one reason: right publisher, wrong page. One
click down, `/docs/devtools/lighthouse` states the task's thesis in Google's own
words (§*Lighthouse is influenced by your setup*, quoted below). Replace the URL,
not the task — and note that `/docs/lighthouse/...` is a **separate top-level docs
section**, not reachable by drilling down from the named page.

## b. Best primary source

**Lighthouse's own `docs/variability.md`** — a rare case where a primary source
addresses the learning goal head-on rather than by implication. The whole document
exists to argue the task's point, and its spine is two tables the lesson can lift
wholesale. **Table 1** rates seven variability sources by impact and by likelihood
in three environments (Typical End User / PageSpeed Insights / Controlled Lab).
**Table 2** rates how well each throttling strategy (Simulated / DevTools / No
Throttling) mitigates each source, in cells reading `NO MITIGATION` / `PARTIALLY
MITIGATED` / `MITIGATED` — the more valuable of the two, because no configuration
removes variance, only redistributes it.

Two official supplements: **Chrome, *Lighthouse performance scoring***
(https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/performance/performance-scoring) —
weights plus §*Why your score fluctuates*, where the learner finds out the score is
a **weighted composite**, so "the score moved" means nothing alone; and **web.dev,
*Lab and field data differences*** (https://web.dev/articles/lab-and-field-data-differences)
— prioritize field data, use lab data to find opportunities, reframing Lighthouse
as diagnostic, not verdict.

## c. What it leaves out

- **A noise threshold.** variability.md quantifies exactly one thing — the 5-run
  median claim — then says only "use aggregate values." No "a 4-point move is
  noise" rule of thumb for a human reading three reports.
- **What "stable finding" means.** Undefined by any source here; the lesson must
  supply it. A finding is stable if it appears in all three runs *with the same
  cause* — not with the same number.
- **The audit list vs. the score.** Both docs are almost entirely about the *number*.
  Neither says the practical thing: the **opportunities and diagnostics list is far
  more stable than the score**, because "render-blocking CSS" is a fact about the
  page and "78" is a measurement. No primary source states it.
- **Category conflation.** Only Performance is stochastic; the Accessibility score is
  near-deterministic and near-meaningless (automated-check coverage, not
  accessibility). No doc warns against averaging these, or says how to neutralize
  A/B tests, ads, or consent banners for a test run.
- **Deliverable mismatch worth fixing:** the plan says *three* runs, the primary
  source recommends *five*. Three is Lighthouse CI's `autorun` default and
  time-boxes well — but say plainly it is below the documented recommendation, and
  that with n=3 the median is just the middle value.

## d. Credible disagreement

**1. Google vs. Google — the lab/field split.** The scoring doc coaches toward
90-100 green; web.dev says prioritize *field* data, because the lab's LCP element
may not be the users' LCP element, labs run cold-cache while real users are warm,
and field CLS accumulates over the whole page lifetime while the lab measures only
load. A green Lighthouse score alongside failing Core Web Vitals in the field is
therefore normal and expected, not a contradiction. Give the learner both.

**2. Zach Leatherman, "The Art of Deception, Lighthouse Score Edition"** —
https://www.zachleat.com/web/lighthouse-deception/. Sarcastic advice for inflating a
score, which makes it the sharpest teaching artifact available. Shows a **30-point
swing (64 → 94) on the same site from hardware alone**. Its four "evil tips" —
beefiest hardware only; run repeatedly and pick the highest; never admit it was a
Desktop score; shout about field data if your audience is wealthy — are each the
exact inverse of the deliverable.

**3. The measurement-theory objection (RST-adjacent).** Michael Bolton,
*Meaningful Metrics* — https://developsense.com/blog/2009/01/meaningful-metrics —
argues for *"inquiry metrics, rather than control metrics"*: numbers that *"prompt
questions about their assumptions"* rather than answer them, treated as *"first-order
approximations… fundamentally limited and fallible."* His warning that teams become
concerned with *"making the metrics look good"* rather than the work is exactly what
a score target does — the reason the task says "without treating one score as the
full answer."

## e. Worked examples worth adapting

- **The deliverable as designed.** Three runs, same URL, same device setting,
  incognito with extensions off. Record per run: four category scores, raw LCP /
  TBT / CLS, top three opportunities. Then circle what did *not* move — the
  opportunity list should be near-identical while the number wobbles.
- **Escalate to the CLI.** From variability.md verbatim:
  `npx -p @lhci/cli lhci collect --url https://example.com -n 5`. It also ships a
  `computeMedianRun` helper (`lighthouse/core/lib/median-run.js`) — show it to make
  the point that *the tool itself ships a median function*.
- **Break it on purpose.** Run clean, then again while playing a video or with a
  build running — hits §*Client Resource Contention* (High impact, `NO MITIGATION`
  under DevTools throttling). A drop the learner *caused* sticks.
- **Table 2 as a prediction exercise.** Seven source names, three throttling columns
  blank; ask which cells say `MITIGATED`. The surprising row is Client Hardware.
- **Write-up shape.** Three columns (Run 1/2/3) × rows per score and metric, plus
  two prose sections: *Stable* (every run, with cause) and *Noise* (moved with no
  code change, **naming the variability source from Table 1**). Requiring the source
  to be named is what stops the summary being a screenshot.

## Verbatim quotes

**variability.md §Summary:** "Lighthouse performance scores will change due to
inherent variability in web and network technologies, even if there hasn't been a
code change."
**§Sources of Variability** (impact in parens): Page Nondeterminism (High) "logic
that is nondeterministic that changes the way a user experiences a page" · Local
Network (High) "packet loss, variable traffic prioritization, and last-mile network
congestion" · Tier-1 Network (Medium) · Web Server (Low) · Client Hardware (High) ·
Client Resource Contention (High) "Other applications running on the same machine …
can cause contention for CPU, memory, and network resources" · Browser
Nondeterminism (Medium).
**§Strategies:** "The median Lighthouse score of 5 runs is twice as stable as 1 run."
· "Use aggregate values like the median, 90th percentile, or even min/max instead of
single test results." · "DO NOT collect multiple Lighthouse reports at the same time
on the same machine."

**Chrome, *Lighthouse performance scoring*.** LH10 weights: FCP 10%, Speed Index
10%, **LCP 25%**, **TBT 30%**, **CLS 25%**. Bands: "0 to 49 (red): Poor" / "50 to 89
(orange): Needs Improvement" / "90 to 100 (green): Good"; "a 'perfect' score of 100
is extremely challenging to achieve and not expected." §*Why your score fluctuates*:
"A lot of the variability in your overall Performance score and metric values is not
due to Lighthouse." · **money quote:** "it might be more useful to think of your
site performance as a distribution of scores, rather than a single number."

**Chrome, *Lighthouse* (DevTools):** "Lighthouse is influenced by your setup,
including other load happening on your device, Chrome extensions, and any device
settings you've stored in cookies, local storage, or similar." · "you cannot
directly compare two Lighthouse audits completed on different machines."
**web.dev, *Lab and field data differences*:** "Lab data is determined by loading a
web page in a controlled environment with a predefined set of network and device
conditions." · "The LCP element identified in a lab test may not be the same LCP
element users see when visiting your page."
**Lighthouse CI, *Getting Started*:** `lhci autorun` will "run Lighthouse 3 times on
each HTML file" — 3 is the tooling default, 5 is the documented recommendation.
*Caveat: the Leatherman and Bolton quotes in §d came through a summarizing fetch —
re-read those two at source before quoting. All others were read directly.*
