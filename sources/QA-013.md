# QA-013 — Verification and validation

> Dossier, not lesson content. Every claim below must be re-read at source
> before it ships in a lesson.

- **Verdict:** WRONG-DOCUMENT
- **Named source:** https://www.istqb.org/certifications/certified-tester-foundation-level
- **Best primary source:** NASA Systems Engineering Handbook (NASA/SP-2016-6105 Rev2) — https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/nasa_systems_engineering_handbook_0.pdf
- **Exact sections to read:** NASA SE Handbook §2.4 (p. 11), the box "Differences between Verification and Validation Testing" (p. 89), §5.4 opening (p. 99), and the paired boxes "Methods of Verification" (p. 93) / "Methods of Validation" (p. 101). Then ISTQB CTFL v4.0 §1.1 + §1.1.1 (p. 15) for the vocabulary this plan is built on.
- **Read time:** ~25 min

## a. Does the named source cover this?

No. It is a certification landing page, not a document.

The URL 403s to automated fetches, so I could not read it directly this session. Search
snippets describe what a landing page describes: what the certification is, that the exam is
"40 multiple-choice questions," that accredited training is available, and that the syllabus
and sample exams download from an "Attachments" tab. It is a signpost to a PDF.

**And the PDF behind it barely covers this either.** In the CTFL v4.0 syllabus (ASTQB-hosted
official copy, 74 pp., 2023-04-21), verification and validation get **three sentences total**:

- §1.1 (p. 15) — the definitional pair, quoted below.
- §1.1.1 (p. 15) — two bullets in the test-objectives list, one starting "Verifying…", one
  "Validating…".
- §3.1 (p. 32) — "Static testing can be applied for both verification and validation."

The Index (p. 74) confirms this is exhaustive: `validation, 15, 33` and `verification, 15,
33` — two page hits each. Both words are in the Chapter 1 keyword list (p. 14), but **no
learning objective covers the distinction**: Chapter 1's are FL-1.1.1 "(K1) Identify typical
test objectives" and FL-1.1.2 "(K2) Differentiate testing from debugging." The syllabus never
contrasts V and V, gives no example of either, and never asks a learner to classify anything.
So the deliverable is not producible from the named source, nor from the document it links
to. A learner given three sentences and twenty examples will guess.

## b. Best primary source

**NASA Systems Engineering Handbook, NASA/SP-2016-6105 Rev2** — free, stable, US-government,
text-searchable PDF. It has a section written for exactly this question:

- **§2.4 "Distinctions between Product Verification and Product Validation" (p. 11)** — half a
  page, and the single most useful passage I found anywhere. It gives the discriminator a
  learner needs to sort examples: verification compares against the *approved requirements
  set*; validation compares against the *ConOps* and stakeholder expectations. The box on
  p. 89 restates the same split for *testing* specifically.
- **Boxes on p. 93 and p. 101, "Methods of Verification" and "Methods of Validation"** —
  the best teaching device in either document, and the reason I rank NASA above ISTQB. Both
  boxes list the **same four methods: Analysis, Demonstration, Inspection, Test.** The activity
  is identical; only the baseline changes. That is the insight that makes a twenty-item sort
  learnable rather than arbitrary.

Pair it with **ISTQB CTFL v4.0 §1.1** for the vocabulary the rest of this plan uses. NASA for
the substance, ISTQB for the words.

## c. What it leaves out

- **Every example is spaceflight hardware** — arming pins, cockpit mock-ups, flight units, test
  pilots. Not one software example. The lesson builds all twenty items from scratch.
- **No "both" category.** NASA's model is sequential, not overlapping: §5.3.1.3 lists
  "Verified product ready for validation: After the product is verified, it will next pass
  through the Product Validation Process." The only source licensing a "both" bucket is CTFL
  §3.1 on static testing — if the deliverable keeps three buckets, that one sentence carries
  all the weight. Check it before relying on it.
- **Assumes artifacts most software teams don't have** — a baselined requirements set, a ConOps
  document, MOEs/MOPs. A team with user stories and a Slack thread must be told what plays the
  ConOps role. Both documents also treat the baseline as given; in practice the hard part of
  validation is that stakeholders disagree about what they wanted.

## d. Credible disagreement

**The two ISTQB glossary definitions are nearly the same sentence** (both quoted below): each
reads "Confirmation by examination and through provision of objective evidence that
[…] requirements have been fulfilled." The only difference is *whose* requirements. Show the
learner that directly — the distinction is not about the activity, it is about the baseline
you hold the product against. (Both from the mirror istqb-glossary.page, because
glossary.istqb.org 403s. **Re-verify in a browser before quoting.**)

**Rapid Software Testing cuts the pie in a different direction.** Bach & Bolton's split is
*testing vs. checking*, and it runs perpendicular to V vs. V. On their definitions, most of
what people file under "verification" *and* much of what they file under "validation" are both
just checking — the useful question is not which baseline you compared against, but whether a
human was evaluating or a machine was confirming a proposition someone already thought to
write down. A different taxonomy, not a refinement of ISTQB's; say so rather than blend them.

Caveat: I found no RST document taking on verification-vs-validation head-on. The above is the
RST distinction from "Testing and Checking Refined," applied here — not their stated position
on V&V.

## e. Worked examples worth adapting

- **Ariane 5 Flight 501 (Lions Inquiry Board, 19 July 1996)** — the canonical "verified but not
  validated" failure, and genuinely about the *specification* being right for the wrong
  vehicle. The SRI software was reused from Ariane 4, and its specification "does not contain
  the Ariane 5 trajectory data as a functional requirement." Every verification check could
  pass and the rocket still explodes. That is the whole lesson in one story. **Sourcing
  caveat:** quoted from https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~arnold/disasters/ariane5rep.html; the
  official ESA PDF (esamultimedia.esa.int/docs/esa-x-1819eng.pdf) is a scanned fax image with
  no text layer and the MIT copy is HTTP-only. Re-verify wording before publishing.
- **NASA's twin method boxes (pp. 93 and 101)** — hand the learner both lists with the headings
  removed and ask which is which. They can't tell; the methods are identical. That failure *is*
  the teaching moment, and it sets up the twenty-item sort.
- **CTFL §1.1.1's own test-objectives list (p. 15)** — two of its eight bullets are already a
  matched V/V pair (quoted below). Free first two items of the twenty, in the plan's own words.
- **NASA Appendices D (p. 201), E (p. 203) and I (p. 216)** — a Requirements Verification
  Matrix, a Validation Requirements Matrix, and a V&V Plan outline. ToC only; I did not read them.
- **Gap:** no source here supplies twenty ready-made software checks. That list has to be
  written, and it is where the lesson's real work is.

## Verbatim quotes

**ISTQB CTFL v4.0 §1.1, What is Testing? (p. 15)** — the entire V&V treatment in the syllabus:
- "Another common misconception about testing is that testing focuses entirely on verifying the test object. Whilst testing involves verification, i.e., checking whether the system meets specified requirements, it also involves validation, which means checking whether the system meets users' and other stakeholders' needs in its operational environment."

**ISTQB CTFL v4.0 §1.1.1, Test Objectives (p. 15)** — two of eight bullets:
- "Verifying whether specified requirements have been fulfilled"
- "Validating whether the test object is complete and works as expected by the stakeholders"

**ISTQB CTFL v4.0 §3.1, Static Testing Basics (p. 32)** — the only "both" statement found:
- "Static testing can be applied for both verification and validation."

**ISTQB CTFL v4.0, Chapter 1 Learning Objectives (p. 14)** — evidence of absence, quoted in full:
- "FL-1.1.1 (K1) Identify typical test objectives / FL-1.1.2 (K2) Differentiate testing from debugging"

**NASA SE Handbook §2.4, Distinctions between Product Verification and Product Validation (p. 11):**
- "From a process perspective, the Product Verification and Product Validation processes may be similar in nature, but the objectives are fundamentally different"
- "Verification of a product shows proof of compliance with requirements—that the product can meet each 'shall' statement as proven though performance of a test, analysis, inspection, or demonstration (or combination of these)."
- "Validation of a product shows that the product accomplishes the intended purpose in the intended environment—that it meets the expectations of the customer and other stakeholders as shown through performance of a test, analysis, inspection, or demonstration."
- "Verification testing relates back to the approved requirements set… Validation relates back to the ConOps document. Validation testing is conducted under realistic conditions (or simulated conditions) on end products for the purpose of determining the effectiveness and suitability of the product for use in mission operations by typical users." (also repeated verbatim in the p. 89 box)
- "The question should be continually asked, 'Are we building the right product for our users and other stakeholders?'"

**NASA SE Handbook §5.4, Product Validation (p. 99):**
- "While verification proves whether 'the product was done right,' validation proves whether 'the right product was done.'"
- "verification provides objective evidence that every 'shall' statement in the requirements document or specification was met, whereas validation is performed for the benefit of the customers and users to ensure that the system functions in the expected manner when placed in the intended environment."

**NASA SE Handbook §5.3.1.3, Outputs (p. 99)** — the sequencing claim:
- "Verified product ready for validation: After the product is verified, it will next pass through the Product Validation Process."

**ISTQB Glossary** (via mirror istqb-glossary.page — RE-VERIFY at glossary.istqb.org):
- verification: "Confirmation by examination and through provision of objective evidence that specified requirements have been fulfilled."
- validation: "Confirmation by examination and through provision of objective evidence that the requirements for a specific intended use or application have been fulfilled."

**Bach & Bolton, "Testing and Checking Refined"** (satisfice.com/blog/archives/856):
- "Testing is the process of evaluating a product by learning about it through experiencing, exploring, and experimenting, which includes to some degree: questioning, study, modeling, observation, inference, etc."
- "Checking is the mechanistic process of verifying propositions about the product."

**Ariane 5 Flight 501 Inquiry Board report, Lions, 19 July 1996** (via UMN mirror — re-verify):
- §3.2: "The failure of the Ariane 501 was caused by the complete loss of guidance and attitude information 37 seconds after start of the main engine ignition sequence (30 seconds after lift-off)." — traced to "specification and design errors in the software of the inertial reference system."
- §2.3: "the SRI specification (which is supposed to be a requirements document for the SRI) does not contain the Ariane 5 trajectory data as a functional requirement."
- §3.1(r): "The specification of the inertial reference system and the tests performed at equipment level did not specifically include the Ariane 5 trajectory data."
