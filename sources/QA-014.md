# QA-014 — Functional testing and UAT: a test *type* and a test *level*, not two alternatives

> Dossier, not lesson content. Every claim below must be re-read at source
> before it ships in a lesson.

## a. Does the named source cover this?

**No.** The plan names the *certification*, not the *syllabus* — different documents at different URLs, and only one of them teaches anything.

The named URL returns **HTTP 403 Forbidden** to automated fetch, confirmed this session. But the access problem is beside the point: even rendered in a browser, `/certifications/certified-tester-foundation-level` is a product page for an exam — what the certification is, exam structure, accreditation, where to book. No treatment of functional testing, none of UAT, no checklist, no sign-off template. A learner sent here cannot produce the deliverable because there is nothing on the page to produce it from.

The syllabus behind it **does** cover the goal, and covers it well — see (b). Swap the link. This is a one-line fix to the plan, not a research gap.

**Fix the `wants` line too.** "How functional testing and UAT answer different questions" is a category error in ISTQB's own scheme, and teaching it as posed will confuse the learner permanently. **Functional testing is a test *type*; acceptance testing is a test *level*.** Orthogonal axes, not two options on one axis — §2.2 says test-type activities "can be performed at every test level", so functional testing is something you do *during* UAT, not instead of it.

The real contrast the task is reaching for is **verification vs validation**: does it match the specification (functional testing's "functional completeness, functional correctness and functional appropriateness") versus does it meet the business need in the real workflow (acceptance testing "focuses on validation"). Reframed that way the lesson works.

## c. What it leaves out

1. **The whole deliverable, as a procedure.** One paragraph defines acceptance testing and never says how to *run* a UAT session, who convenes it, or what a sign-off note contains. §5.3.2's completion report is generic test reporting, not a business acceptance record.
2. **The unit of account is wrong.** §4.5.2's acceptance criteria are scoped to *a single user story*. The deliverable asks for **one common business flow** (order-to-cash, onboarding) spanning dozens of stories and several systems. The syllabus has no construct at that grain; the learner must invent the bridge.
3. **What signing actually means.** Who signs, what they attest to, and what happens when the business signs off *over* the tester's objection. §5.1.3 gets one clause from the truth — stakeholders "reviewed and accepted the risk" — then drops it.
4. **No template and no worked example anywhere in 78 pages.** Not one.
5. **UAT test data and environment.** Real business flows need realistic data and a production-like environment; test data appears only as an entry criterion.
6. **The actual templates are paywalled.** §5.3.2 points at ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-3 for report templates. That standard is not free — don't send a learner there.

## d. Credible disagreement

**Michael Bolton, "User Acceptance Testing – A Context-Driven Perspective"** (PNSQC 2007) — https://www.developsense.com/presentations/2007-10-PNSQC-UserAcceptanceTesting.pdf — 13 pages, free, and a sustained attack on exactly this deliverable. Read with **James Bach, "Justifying Real Acceptance Testing"** — https://www.satisfice.com/blog/archives/973.

These do not blend with ISTQB. The conflicts are specific:

- **Whether "UAT" means anything.** ISTQB defines it as one form of one test level. Bolton lists **thirteen** incompatible meanings in live use (p.2) and asks whether the term "is effectively meaningless."
- **Users doing the testing.** ISTQB: "Ideally, acceptance testing should be performed by the intended users." Bolton: you are "asking someone who is unlikely to have testing skills to find bugs that they're unlikely to find, at the very time when we're least likely to fix them" (p.8).
- **What UAT usually *is*.** Bolton: much of it is "not testing at all, but a ceremony," where "the expert tester shows his expertise by *never finding a bug*" (p.6).
- **Aimed straight at this deliverable.** "A decision rule says yes or no; a test is information gathering. Many people who want UAT are seeking decision rules" (p.13). A UAT checklist plus sign-off note *is* a decision rule. Not automatically wrong — but the lesson should say so out loud rather than pretend the artifact is an investigation.
- **Acceptance criteria as a test basis** (undercuts §4.5.2): user stories "form a pretty weak basis for testing... they tend to exercise happy workflows and downplay error conditions and exception handling" (p.8).
- **Bach, on who must hold the pen:** "Only the accepting party has the incentive to test well" — vendor-run acceptance testing is a conflict of interest.

Bolton is explicit that refusing the job is not the answer: context-driven testers "shouldn't panic and attempt to weasel out of the service role" (p.13). So the lesson can still teach the checklist — it just has to name which of the thirteen games is being played.

## e. Worked examples worth adapting

- **Bolton's opening anecdote (p.2)** — one banking-software organization used "user acceptance testing" for *three different activities at once*: per-story examples, a month of harsh in-house testing, and a month of testing at the client banks. Best possible cold open.
- **The 13-definition list (p.2)** — hand it to the learner, ask which one their employer means. That exercise *is* the learning goal.
- **Bolton's mode taxonomy (pp.6–12)** — ceremony, demonstration, smoke test, mild exercise, validation, assigning blame, user-is-other-software, beta, examples, milestones, probing. Classification drill: label a described UAT phase.
- **The Queen inspecting the troops (p.7)** and **the new-car test drive (p.7)** — two short, memorable analogies for ceremonial inspection, reusable close to verbatim.
- **TSB, April 2018** — a real, documented, business-flow-scale sign-off failure: go-live was approved with a large open-defect backlog and incomplete non-functional testing. Primary source: Slaughter and May's independent review, published by TSB — https://www.tsb.co.uk/content/dam/tsb-public/documents/media-centre/Slaughter-and-May-final-report.pdf (landing page: https://www.tsb.co.uk/news-releases/slaughter-and-may.html). **The specific defect counts circulating in press coverage were NOT verified against the report this session — read the PDF before quoting any number.** Pair with §5.1.3's "stakeholders have reviewed and accepted the risk to go live": TSB is what that sentence looks like when the review is not independent.

## Verbatim quotes

**CTFL v4.0.1 §2.2 (p.28):** "Test types are groups of test activities related to specific quality characteristics and most of those test activities can be performed at every test level."

**§2.2.1 (p.29):** "**Acceptance testing** focuses on validation and on demonstrating readiness for deployment, which means that the system fulfills the user's business needs. Ideally, acceptance testing should be performed by the intended users. The main forms of acceptance testing are: user acceptance testing (UAT), operational acceptance testing, contractual acceptance testing and regulatory acceptance testing, alpha testing and beta testing."

**§2.2.2 (p.29):** "**Functional testing** evaluates the functions that a component or system should perform. The functions are "what" the test object should do. The main objective of functional testing is checking the functional completeness, functional correctness and functional appropriateness."

**§4.4.3 (p.44):** "Checklists should not contain items that can be checked automatically, items better suited as entry criteria, exit criteria, or items that are too general (Brykczynski 1999)."

**§4.4.3 (p.45):** "Checklist items are often phrased in the form of a question. It should be possible to check each item separately and directly."

**§5.1.3 (p.49):** "Exit criteria define what must be achieved to declare an activity completed." … "Running out of time or budget can also be viewed as valid exit criteria. Even without other exit criteria being satisfied, it can be acceptable to end testing under such circumstances, if the stakeholders have reviewed and accepted the risk to go live without further testing."

**§5.3.2 (p.55):** Test completion reports typically include "Test summary … Deviations from the test plan … **Unmitigated risks, defects not fixed** … Lessons learned that are relevant to the testing"

**Bolton, PNSQC 2007 (p.6):** "Acceptance testing is any testing done by one party for the purpose accepting another party's work. It's whatever the tester and the acceptor agree upon; whatever the key is to open the gate for acceptance—however secure or ramshackle the lock."

**Bolton (p.6):** "In some cases, UAT is not testing at all, but a ceremony." … "for this kind of dog and pony show, the expert tester shows his expertise by *never finding a bug*."

**Bolton (p.8):** "At this stage, we're asking someone who is unlikely to have testing skills to find bugs that they're unlikely to find, at the very time when we're least likely to fix them."

**Bolton (p.13):** "A decision rule says yes or no; a test is information gathering. Many people who want UAT are seeking decision rules."

**Bach, "Justifying Real Acceptance Testing":** acceptance testing is "testing by a potential acceptor (a customer), performed for the purpose of informing a decision to accept a product." … "Only the accepting party has the incentive to test well."
