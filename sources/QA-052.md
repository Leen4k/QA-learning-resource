## a. Does the named source cover this?

**No — and this time the absence is measurable.** I fetched `roadmap.sh/qa`
this session: the page carries navigation, a one-line "QA Engineer roadmap for
2026" blurb, share buttons, community counts and a footer. No prose on
reporting.

The node blurbs live in the roadmap's content repo, so I checked there too.
`src/data/roadmaps/qa/content` in `kamranahmedse/developer-roadmap` holds
**101 files**, and **not one filename contains `report`, `metric`, `document`,
`summar`, `risk`, or `defect`.** The graph covers tools (cypress, playwright,
jest, jmeter, postman, selenium), test types (accessibility, performance, load,
exploratory), process (agile, bdd, atdd, kanban) and CI/monitoring (jenkins,
datadog, grafana, pagerduty). Communicating results is simply not a node.

So for a 6h Must-do capstone whose whole deliverable is *a published report*,
the plan points at a document with zero coverage of the deliverable. Replace
the line.

## b. Best primary source

**ISTQB CTFL v4.0 §5.3.2, "Purpose, Content and Audience for Test Reports."**
Its test-completion-report content list maps onto this task's three verbs
almost one-to-one, which is rare enough to be worth calling out:

| Task wants | §5.3.2 bullet |
|---|---|
| "what I tested" | *Test summary* · *Test metrics based on test progress reports* |
| "what remains risky" | *Unmitigated risks, defects not fixed* |
| "what I learned" | *Lessons learned that are relevant to the testing* |

§5.3.1 supplies the six metric families to draw the numbers from; §5.3.3 covers
the delivery channel and makes the audience point explicit. It is a page and a
half — appropriate, because the 6h belongs in the writing, not the reading.

**Boundary with existing dossiers.** QA-049 already lists §5.3–5.3.2 as a
companion, and QA-014 cites §5.3.2 for defect reporting. QA-052 owns the
*completion report* half of §5.3.2 — the once-per-project artifact — and must
not re-teach progress reporting or defect reports.

## c. What it leaves out

- **No template and no example.** §5.3.2 ends by deferring: *"The ISO/IEC/IEEE
  29119-3 standard includes templates and examples..."* — a standard sold by
  ISO and IEEE, not free. Confirmed listings: https://www.iso.org/standard/56737.html
  and https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/29119-3/7499/. A 2013 copy is mirrored on
  a university course site (`wildart.github.io/MISG5020/standards/`); treat that
  as unlicensed and do not build the lesson on it. **The learner gets a
  seven-item shopping list and no worked artifact — that gap is the lesson's
  main job.**
- **No "what we did not test."** The completion-report list has *Unmitigated
  risks, defects not fixed* but no item for coverage never attempted. See (d).
- **Nothing on the second half of the deliverable.** "Ask someone to review the
  evidence and remaining risks" has no counterpart in §5.3 — no guidance on
  getting a report peer-reviewed, or on what a reviewer should push back on.
- **No help phrasing residual risk** so a stakeholder can act on it. One bullet.
- **Metrics without a warning label.** §5.3.1 lists *"number of test cases
  run/not run, passed/failed"* neutrally, with no caution about what those
  numbers do to a reader. That is precisely the disputed ground.
- **Nothing about a portfolio.** The task title says "portfolio review"; ISTQB
  has no notion of career evidence. That framing has to come from the lesson.

## d. Credible disagreement

**Michael Bolton / RST, on two specific points — not a general vibe.**

1. **Against pass/fail counts as the substance of a report.** Bolton, *Braiding
   The Stories* (2012-02-24): pass-vs-fail ratios *"come from the fake world."*
   ISTQB lists exactly that ratio as a test progress metric feeding the
   completion report. RST's replacement is qualitative: *"Managers need to know
   about problems that threaten the value of the product... That knowledge
   requires more than abstract data; it requires information."*

2. **ISTQB's list has no strand for "was our testing any good?"** RST's report
   has three: the product story, the testing story (*"how we configured,
   operated, observed, and evaluated the product"*), and the quality-of-testing
   story (*"why the testing we've done has been good enough, and why the testing
   we haven't done hasn't been so important so far"*). ISTQB's *"Testing and
   product quality evaluation based on the original test plan"* measures testing
   against the plan — not whether the testing itself was any good.

Bolton also insists on the negative space, which ISTQB's list omits outright:
*"It may be far more important to talk about what we haven't covered yet, or
won't cover at all unless something changes."* — *How is the testing going?*
(2018-02). Reconciliation for the lesson: use ISTQB's seven headings as the
skeleton, add an explicit "not covered, and why" section, and write each heading
as prose rather than a count.

## e. Worked examples worth adapting

**James Bach, *A Low-Tech Testing Dashboard* (STAR '99 East, 16 slides,
~10 min).** The single most adaptable artifact found. It is a complete, filled-in
report table with real values and full legends — exactly the worked example
ISTQB withholds.

- Columns (slide 8): **Area | Effort | C. | Q. | Comments**, headed
  `Updated: 2/21  Build: 38`. Sample rows: `tools | blocked | 1 | ☹ | crashes:
  1406, 1407`; `online help | blocked | 0 | | new files not delivered`.
- **Effort** scale (slide 10): None / Start / Low / High / Pause / Blocked /
  Ship, each with a one-line definition.
- **Coverage** scale (slide 12): `0` *"We have no good information about this
  area."* → `1` Sanity Check → `1+` → `2` Common Cases → `2+` → `3` Corner Cases.
- **Quality** (slide 14): three faces, each a full sentence, e.g. red =
  *"We know of problems in this area that definitely stop ship or interrupt
  testing."*
- Area-selection rules (slide 9) transfer directly to "what I tested": 15–30
  areas, no sub-areas, roughly equal value, minimal overlap, collectively
  inclusive, and *"Areas must 'make sense' to your clients."*
- Slide 16 is the peer-review hook for this task's second half:
  *"Justification: Be ready to justify the contents of any cell in the
  dashboard."*

**Also worth mining, unread this session:** Bach & Bach, *Session-Based Test
Management* (2000-11-01, rev. 2021-07-07, PDF 174 KB) —
https://www.satisfice.com/download/session-based-test-management. The page
confirms the file's existence and authorship; the session-sheet and debrief
detail must be read before it is cited.

## Verbatim quotes

- **CTFL v4.0 §5.3.2, p.54** — completion report contents: *"Test summary · Testing and product quality evaluation based on the original test plan (i.e., test objectives and exit criteria) · Deviations from the test plan (e.g., differences from the planned schedule, duration, and effort). · Testing impediments and workarounds · Test metrics based on test progress reports · Unmitigated risks, defects not fixed · Lessons learned that are relevant to the testing"*
