## b. Best primary source

CTFL has no chapter called "run a test cycle", but four sections assemble into an exact spine,
and it is the only free authoritative document naming every artifact the task asks for:

| Deliverable step | Section |
|---|---|
| run the cases, log the results | §1.4.1 *Test execution* |
| what you must keep | §1.4.3 *Testware* — "test logs, and defect reports" |

§5.5's field list is the most directly usable page in the syllabus for this task: a ready-made

format.

## c. What it leaves out

- **No test case template. Anywhere.** I read §1.4.1-1.4.5, §5.1.1-5.1.5 and §5.3-5.5; none
  shows what a test case looks like on the page. §1.4.3 lists "(prioritized) test cases" as a
  work product and stops. The task says *write and run twenty of them.*
- **No test log format either.** §1.4.3 names "test logs"; nothing says what a row contains.
  Both gaps defer to **ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-3**, cited three times (§5.1.1, §5.3.2, §5.5) — a
  paid ISO standard. iso.org 403'd me this session, so do not describe its contents from memory.
- **Nothing on evidence for a *passing* case.** "test logs, database dumps, screenshots, or
  recordings" appears only inside §5.5's defect-report list. What to capture when a case
  passes — 80% of a 20-case run — is unaddressed.
- **"Twenty" is the plan author's number, not the standard's.** §5.1.3's exit criteria are
  coverage-, defect- and completion-shaped, never a raw case count. Say the 20 is scaffolding.
- **"Repeatable" is asserted, not taught.** §5.4 says CM makes it "possible to revert to a
  previous baseline to reproduce previous test results" and gives no procedure. The second
  run — the thing that makes it a *cycle* — has no section.
- **Severity vs priority get one line each**, no scale, no example. Learners conflate them.
  And **confirmation testing of your own defects** sits in §2.2.3, outside every section above.

## d. Credible disagreement

**Bolton attacks this deliverable's premise by name.** "Breaking the Test Case Addiction"
(Part 1, developsense.com, Jan 2019): "testing is not test cases; testing is a performance",
and counting them "leads to no end of mischief" because "Tests—performances of testing
activity—become reified, turned into things, widgets, testburgers." Also: "A mandate to follow
a script removes agency, turning the tester into a robot instead of an investigator." "Run at
least twenty manual cases" is precisely the testburger metric. Say so rather than smoothing it.

**But the RST alternative is not "keep no evidence" — it is a different unit of account.**
Bach's SBTM replaces the case with the **session**: "a session, not a test case or bug report,
is the basic testing work unit", ~90 minutes, chartered, always producing a reviewable session
sheet. The argument is case-vs-session, not documented-vs-undocumented. Teachable resolution:
charter the work in sessions, list the twenty cases as *coverage* inside the sheet, report both.

SBTM also **criticises itself**, worth showing: "the session-based testing process and
associated metrics could easily be distorted by a confused or biased test manager", and a
section headed *"Seems like too much bureaucracy"* concedes the structure "is not strictly
necessary in order to achieve the mission of good testing."

**Kaner rejects ISTQB's framing of the defect report as a record.** §5.5: reports "provide
those responsible for handling and resolving reported defects with sufficient information to
resolve the issue." Kaner: "Think of a bug report as a tool that you use to sell the company on
the idea of spending time and energy to fix a bug", and "The best tester is the one who gets the
most bugs fixed." Anti-metric in the same breath: "I'm not encouraging bug counting metrics.
These are almost always counterproductive."

## e. Worked examples worth adapting

- **The MapMaker session sheet, printed in full in the SBTM article (pp. 4-6):** CHARTER,
  #AREAS, START, TESTER, TASK BREAKDOWN, DATA FILES, TEST NOTES, BUGS, ISSUES — with real
  entries ("#BUG 1321 Zooming in makes you put in the CD 2 … even if CD 2 is already in the
  drive"). Hand it over as the evidence template; it beats anything in CTFL for concreteness.
- **PROOF debrief** — Past, Results, Obstacles, Outlook, Feelings. A five-minute sign-off ritual
  to close each cycle, from someone who ran it on a real project.
- **SBTM's effort reality check:** their published breakdown was Test 28%, Bug 4%, Setup 6%,
  Opportunity 1%, **Non-Session 61%** — "the testers are spending only about a third of their
  time actually testing." Against an 8-hour capstone that predicts ~2.5h of real execution.
- **Kaner's follow-up triad** — vary my behavior / vary the options and settings / vary the
  software and hardware environment — turns a flat 20-case pass into real investigation, and is
  the practical route to "more serious and more general than it first appears." Paired rule:
  **"IT PAYS TO REPLICATE EVERY BUG ON A SECOND MACHINE"** — catches config-dependent bugs
  before the programmer rejects them as irreproducible.
- **Kaner's 2-minute opener:** the program `INPUT A / INPUT B / PRINT A/B` — "What is the fault?
  What is the critical condition? What will we see as the failure?" Separates fault, condition
  and failure before the learner writes a single report.
- **Bugzilla's Bug Writing Guidelines** (https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/page.cgi?id=bug-writing.html)
  — "Steps to reproduce are the most important part of any bug report", "Open a new bug report
  for each issue!", and a 60-character summary limit that is a genuine writing exercise.

## Verbatim quotes

ISTQB quotes from **CTFL v4.0 GA, 2023-04-21**, printed page numbers.

**§1.4.1 (p.19):** "Test execution may be manual or automated. … Actual test results are
compared with the expected results. The test results are logged. Anomalies are analyzed to
identify their likely causes." · "A test completion report is created and communicated to the
stakeholders."

**§1.4.3 (p.20):** "Test execution work products include: test logs, and defect reports (see
section 5.5)."

**§5.1.3 (p.48):** "Exit criteria define what must be achieved in order to declare an activity
completed." · "Typical exit criteria include: measures of thoroughness (e.g., achieved level of
coverage, number of unresolved defects, defect density, number of failed test cases), and
completion criteria (e.g., planned tests have been executed, static testing has been performed,
all defects found are reported, all regression tests are automated)." · "Running out of time or
budget can also be viewed as valid exit criteria." · "In Agile software development, exit
criteria are often called Definition of Done."

**§5.3.1 (p.53):** "Test progress metrics (e.g., … number of test cases run/not run,
passed/failed, test execution time)" · "Defect metrics (e.g., number and priorities of defects
found/fixed, defect density, defect detection percentage)".

**§5.3.2 (p.54):** "Typical test completion reports include: Test summary · Testing and product
quality evaluation based on the original test plan (i.e., test objectives and exit criteria) ·
Deviations from the test plan … · Testing impediments and workarounds · Test metrics based on
test progress reports · Unmitigated risks, defects not fixed · Lessons learned."

**§5.4 (p.55):** "It is possible to revert to a previous baseline to reproduce previous test
results."

**§5.5 (p.55-56), defect report fields:** "Unique identifier · Title with a short summary of the
anomaly being reported · Date when the anomaly was observed, issuing organization, and author,
including their role · Identification of the test object and test environment · Context of the
defect · Description of the failure to enable reproduction and resolution including the steps
that detected the anomaly, and any relevant test logs, database dumps, screenshots, or
recordings · Expected results and actual results · Severity of the defect (degree of impact) …
· Priority to fix · Status of the defect (e.g., open, deferred, duplicate, waiting to be fixed,
awaiting confirmation testing, re-opened, closed, rejected) · References (e.g., to the test
case)."

**Jonathan Bach, SBTM (2000):** "a session, not a test case or bug report, is the basic testing
work unit." · "What we call a session is an uninterrupted block of reviewable, chartered test
effort." · "In my team, sessions last 90 minutes, give or take." · Report sections: "Session
charter … Tester name(s) · Date and time started · Task breakdown (the TBS metrics) · Data
files · Test notes · Issues · Bugs."

**Cem Kaner, Bug Advocacy (2002):** "Bug reports are your primary work product." · "The best
tester isn't the one who finds the most bugs or who embarrasses the most programmers. The best
tester is the one who gets the most bugs fixed." · "a bug report is successful if it enables an
informed business decision." · "In mass-market software, most of the bugs customers call to
complain about were discovered during testing but not fixed."

**Michael Bolton, Breaking the Test Case Addiction, Part 1 (2019):** "testing is not test cases;
testing is a performance." · "Effort becomes recast in terms of counting test cases, which leads
to no end of mischief." · "Tests—performances of testing activity—become reified, turned into
things, widgets, testburgers."
