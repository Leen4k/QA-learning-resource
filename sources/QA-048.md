## a. Does the named source cover this?

**No. It is a project-listing page, and its one project is a different task.**
Fetched this session, `roadmap.sh/qa/projects` carries no prose on strategy,
scope, risk, environments, or evidence. It lists exactly one project: *"Writing
an end-to-end (e2e) test cases for an e-commerce app."* I followed that to the
brief (`roadmap.sh/projects/e2e-test-ecommerce-app`): test against
`bookcart.azurewebsites.net`, automate four flows — login/registration, search,
cart, checkout — pick Playwright/Cypress/Selenium, add assertions, add a
reporter. Against this DELIVERABLE it scores: **scope** — handed over as four
bullets, not derived; **risks** — absent; **environments** — one hard-coded URL;
**evidence** — *"implement a reporting tool"*, a test-runner artifact, not a
report to a stakeholder. A learner who finishes it has written tests, not a
strategy. Replace the plan line.

## b. Best primary source

**The HTSM, and the reason is a finding, not a preference.** CTFL v4.0's Chapter
5 keyword list (p.46) runs *"…test approach, test completion report, test
control, test monitoring, test plan, test planning, test progress report, test
pyramid, testing quadrants"* — **"test strategy" is not in it**, and no learning
objective FL-5.x covers strategy. §5.1.1 only says a plan *"Demonstrates that
testing will adhere to the existing test policy and test strategy"* — it assumes
a strategy exists upstream and never says how to write one. The syllabus cannot
be primary for a task whose noun is *strategy*. The HTSM is *"a set of patterns
for designing and choosing tests to perform"* — four guideword lists that map
onto this deliverable almost one-to-one:

- **scope** → *Test Items → Scope*: "What parts of the product are and are not within the scope of your testing responsibility?"
- **risks** → *Quality Criteria Categories* ("categories of product risk") + *Risk Testing*
- **environments** → *Platform* + *Equipment & Tools* + *Test Items → Availability*
- **evidence** → *Deliverables* + "The Testing and Quality Story"

Use ISTQB alongside for the skeleton and vocabulary the learner meets at work.
**QA-006** already anchors on §5.1 — QA-048 must not re-teach that content list.
Its job is the strategy *behind* it.

## c. What it leaves out

- **Environments, from both.** ISTQB gives "test environment requirements" as a
  parenthetical in one bullet (§5.1.1), "test environment preparation progress"
  as a metric (§5.3.1), and an environment as a configuration item (§5.4). HTSM's
  *Platform* is a thinking prompt. **Neither describes a web environment
  topology** — local/CI/staging/production, seeded vs. anonymised data,
  third-party sandboxes, feature flags, ownership. Fowler's *DeploymentPipeline*
  is the nearest free framing but never says "test environment".
- **What a *web* product makes risky.** Neither is web-specific: no
  browser/device matrix, auth/session/CSRF, payment sandbox, third-party outage,
  cookie-consent path.
- **Sizing, and sign-off.** Neither says how long a strategy should be (ISTQB's
  list runs seven headings, HTSM ~100 guidewords), who approves it, or what
  happens when it is ignored. `reference/test-plan-one-pager.html` in this repo
  is the antidote to the bloat.
- **A worked example.** Neither contains a filled-in strategy. ISTQB defers to
  ISO/IEC/IEEE 29119-3, paywalled — don't send a self-funded learner there.

## d. Credible disagreement

**Sharp, and it lands on the "evidence" half of this deliverable.** Bach &
Bolton's *A Rapid Introduction to Rapid Software Testing* (fetched this session)
has a slide headed **"Uninteresting Testing Questions"**: *"Does this test case
pass or fail? How many test cases do we have? What's our pass/fail ratio?…"*
Every one is an ISTQB §5.3.1 metric (*"number of test cases run/not run,
passed/failed"*) or a §5.1.3 exit criterion. RST's counter-proposal is the
**testing story**: per the HTSM, *"You can never know the 'actual' quality of a
software product— you can't 'verify' quality, as such— but by performing tests,
you can make an assessment, and that takes the form of a story you tell."*
On form, RST stamps a numbered scripted procedure **"THIS IS NOT TESTING / THIS
IS OBSESSIVE DEMONSTRATION"**, and defines testing as *"evaluating a product by
learning about it through exploration and experimentation"* — a direct challenge
to a strategy written as a pre-committed inventory of tests. **Don't blend them.**
Teach the ISTQB skeleton as the form a strategy usually takes at work, then the
RST question — *what story will this evidence let me tell, and would I bet on
it?* — as the test of whether it was filled in honestly.

## e. Worked examples worth adapting

- **Run the HTSM against the roadmap.sh sample app.** `bookcart.azurewebsites.net`
  is live, free, and already in the plan's own resource. Walk *Product Elements*
  over it, then *Quality Criteria* — Capability, Reliability, Usability,
  Charisma, Security, Scalability, Compatibility, Performance, Installability,
  Development — marking each **relevant / not relevant, and why**. That one pass
  produces the scope and risk sections of the deliverable.
- **Risk Testing as a drill** (HTSM p.2): *"1. What kinds of problems could the
  product have? 2. Which kinds matter most? Focus on those. 3. How would you
  detect them if they were there? 4. Make a list of interesting problems and
  design tests specifically to reveal them."*
- **Project Environment questions as a stakeholder interview script:** *"Have you
  negotiated project conditions that affect your ability to accept your
  mission?"* · *"Will you test in production?"* · *"What sort of reports will you
  have to make?"*
- **Two-column risk table** (§5.2.2): project risks (organizational, people,
  technical, supplier) beside product risks, scored by likelihood × impact, then
  §5.2.4's menu — *"risk mitigation by testing, risk acceptance, risk transfer,
  or contingency plan."* Force a **stop rule** for each row.
- **Write the completion report first.** Hand the learner §5.3.2's list and have
  them draft it *before* the strategy — it forces the evidence section to be real.
- **No real defect story found.** I fetched none this session. Do not invent one.

## Verbatim quotes

**HTSM v6.0, p.1:** "The Heuristic Test Strategy Model is a set of patterns for
designing and choosing tests to perform." · "Project Environment represents a set
of context factors that include resources, constraints, and other elements in the
project that may enable or hobble our testing." · "Quality Criteria Categories
are dimensions in which people determine the value of the product. You can also
think of them as categories of product risk." · "You can never know the "actual"
quality of a software product— you can't "verify" quality, as such— but by
performing tests, you can make an assessment, and that takes the form of a story
you tell." · **p.3:** "*Availability*: Do you have the product to test? Do you
have test platforms available? Will you test in production?" · "*Content*: What
sort of reports will you have to make?" · **p.4:** "Platform. Everything on which
the product depends (and that is outside your project)."

**ISTQB CTFL v4.0 §5.1.1 (p.47):** "A test plan describes the objectives,
resources and processes for a test project." · a plan "Demonstrates that testing

testing will deviate from them)". · "The typical content of a test plan includes:
Context of testing (e.g., scope, test objectives, constraints, test basis);
Assumptions and constraints…; Stakeholders…; Communication…; Risk register (e.g.,
product risks, project risks); Test approach (e.g., test levels, test types, test
techniques, test deliverables, entry criteria and exit criteria, independence of
testing, metrics to be collected, test data requirements, test environment

activities are selected, prioritized, and managed based on risk analysis and risk

progress metrics (e.g., … number of test cases run/not run, passed/failed…)" ·
