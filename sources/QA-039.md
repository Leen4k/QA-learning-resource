# QA-039 — Security testing and OWASP

> Dossier, not lesson content. Every claim below must be re-read at source
> before it ships in a lesson.

- **Verdict:** WRONG-DOCUMENT
- **Named source:** https://owasp.org/www-project-top-ten/
- **Best primary source:** OWASP *Web Security Testing Guide* v4.2, §4.5 Authorization Testing — https://owasp.org/www-project-web-security-testing-guide/stable/4-Web_Application_Security_Testing/05-Authorization_Testing/
- **Exact sections to read:** WSTG v4.2 §4.5.2 *Testing for Bypassing Authorization Schema* and §4.5.4 *Testing for Insecure Direct Object References* (skim §4.5.1, §4.5.3) · WSTG `checklists/checklist.md` · OWASP Top 10:2025 **A01:2025 Broken Access Control** · Top 10 §*How to use the OWASP Top 10 as a standard*
- **Read time:** ~50 min

## a. Does the named source cover this?

No. It is a **project portal**, not a document. Fetched this session, its headings are:
Translation Efforts · Completed Translations · Historic · 2021/2017/2003-2013 Project
Sponsors · 2025 Data Analysis Plan · Contribution Process · Data Structure · Survey.
It contains **no risk list, no risk descriptions, no testing guidance, no checklist**
— the content lives one click away at `owasp.org/Top10/`.

The charitable reading — that the plan meant `owasp.org/Top10/2025/` — still fails the
DELIVERABLE, and OWASP says so itself: the Top 10 "documents AppSec risks, and **not
necessarily easily testable issues**," and is "the bare minimum and just a starting
point." A01:2025 gives *Description*, *How to Prevent* and three *Example Attack
Scenarios* — zero *how to test*. It covers the first half of `wants` and none of the
second, and nowhere says where testing is legal.

## b. Best primary source

**OWASP WSTG v4.2 §4.5 Authorization Testing** (stable, 3 Dec 2020) — same foundation,
the testing project rather than the awareness project. Four sub-sections: 4.5.1
Directory Traversal File Include · **4.5.2 Bypassing Authorization Schema** · 4.5.3
Privilege Escalation · **4.5.4 Insecure Direct Object References**. §4.5.2's objective
is literally the task — *"Assess if horizontal or vertical access is possible"* — and
it supplies both authorization cases as complete HTTP request/response pairs (§e).

For the checklist half the artifact exists:
`github.com/OWASP/wstg/tree/master/checklists` — `checklist.md`, `checklist.xlsx`,
`WSTG-Checklist_v4.2.xlsx`, with *Testing Checklist*, *Summary Findings* and *Risk
Assessment Calculator* sheets. Have the learner **cut it down to their app**, not
copy it. Pair with **A01:2025** for risk vocabulary and the prevention side, which
WSTG §4.5 reduces to one sentence.

## c. What it leaves out

- **Where it is legal to test.** WSTG §4.5 assumes an already-authorised target. The
  `wants` line says "safely"; the source says nothing about scope, permission, or who
  to ask — this gap is the lesson's real content.
- **v4.2 is from December 2020.** A01:2025 lists JWT/cookie tampering, CORS
  misconfiguration and "APIs missing controls for POST, PUT, DELETE" as common
  access-control failures; §4.5 has no test procedure for any of them. Say the
  checklist is form-and-cookie era rather than letting them assume it complete.
- **Tooling.** Every §4.5.2 example is a raw HTTP request with a swapped `Cookie:
  SessionID=`. Producing one needs an intercepting proxy (Burp/ZAP) or devtools; the
  guide never teaches it.
- **How to write the finding up.** No severity model in §4.5, no repro-steps format, no
  bridge from "the response contained another user's data" to a QA defect report. The
  Risk Assessment Calculator sheet is the nearest thing.
- **How to build a checklist**, as opposed to receive one — scoping ~100 generic WSTG
  items down to the dozen that apply to one app is the actual skill. §4.5.2's
  Remediation is one line; pull A01:2025 *How to Prevent* for the fix side.

## d. Credible disagreement

**OWASP contradicts this task's own deliverable.** The plan says "make a security
checklist" from the Top Ten; *How to use the OWASP Top 10 as a standard* says don't
(quotes below). A ten-line Top-10 checklist a tester ticks and calls done is the
precise failure mode OWASP names. Resolve it: build the checklist from **WSTG test
IDs** (testable), use the **Top 10 for vocabulary**, cite **ASVS** for a standard.

**Second split: scanners vs. a human who knows the business rules.** A01:2025 reports
*100% of applications tested* had some form of broken access control and recommends
*"Include functional access testing in unit and integration tests."* No scanner knows
Alice must not see Bob's invoice — that lives in the requirements, which is why this
belongs to QA and not only to a pentest vendor. Frame the two-user session swap as a
**regression test QA owns**.

*Unverified lead:* Rapid Software Testing's objection to checklists as a substitute for
thinking would land here — not fetched this session, so do not quote until read.

## e. Worked examples worth adapting

- **Case 1 — horizontal, WSTG §4.5.2.** Two equal-privilege users, two live sessions;
  same body `username=example_user`, swapped `SessionID`, against `POST
  /account/viewSettings`. Ships the vulnerable 200 with the victim's email and address.
- **Case 2 — vertical, same section.** The banking role table (Administrator: Full
  Control/Delete · Manager: Modify, Add, Read · Staff: Read, Modify · Customer: Read
  Only), then `POST /account/deleteEvent` with `EventID=1000001` replayed under a
  Customer session, expected `{"message": "Event was deleted"}`.
- **30-second bonus, same section.** Baseline `GET /`, re-send with `X-Original-URL:
  /donotexist1` / `X-Rewrite-URL: /donotexist2`; a 404 means the app honours the header
  and `/admin` may be reachable through it.
- **Four IDOR shapes, §4.5.4** — a ready checklist skeleton: record (`?invoice=12345`),
  operation (`?user=someuser`), file (`?img=img00011`), function (`?menuitem=12`).
- **Safe environment, ranked.** (1) **OWASP Juice Shop** in local Docker — the learner
  owns the target outright. Its Broken Access Control chapter has **View Basket** ⭐⭐
  *"View another user's shopping basket"* (horizontal) and **Admin Section** ⭐⭐
  *"Access the administration section of the store"* (vertical) — the two cases,
  exactly. The free companion guide at pwning.owasp-juice.shop has step-by-step
  solutions, so release those only after the attempt. (2) **PortSwigger Web Security
  Academy**, hosted and sanctioned: *Lab: User ID controlled by request parameter*
  (creds `wiener:peter`, *"Obtain the API key for the user carlos"*) and *Lab:
  Unprotected admin functionality* (*"Solve the lab by deleting the user carlos"*).
  Free; solving needs an account.

## Verbatim quotes

**Named source**, entire substantive content: the Top Ten "represents a broad consensus
about the most critical security risks to web applications."

**WSTG §4.5.2:** "This kind of test focuses on verifying how the authorization schema
has been implemented for each role or privilege to get access to reserved functions
and resources." · Objectives: "Assess if horizontal or vertical access is possible." ·
"If the attacker's response contain the data of the example_user, then the application
is vulnerable for lateral movement attacks." · Remediation: "Employ the least privilege
principles on the users, roles, and resources…"

**WSTG §4.5.4:** "Insecure Direct Object References (IDOR) occur when an application
provides direct access to objects based on user-supplied input." · Objectives: "Identify
points where object references may occur" / "Assess the access control measures and if
they're vulnerable to IDOR."

**A01:2025:** "Maintaining its position at #1 in the Top Ten, 100% of the applications
tested" had some form of broken access control · 40 CWEs, 1,839,701 occurrences, 32,654
CVEs · Prevent: "Access control is only effective when implemented in trusted server-side
code"; deny by default; "Include functional access testing in unit and integration tests."

***How to use the OWASP Top 10 as a standard*:** "The OWASP Top 10 is primarily an
awareness document." · "the bare minimum and just a starting point." · it documents
"AppSec risks, and not necessarily easily testable issues." · "We would encourage
anyone wanting to adopt an application security standard to use the OWASP Application
Security Verification Standard (ASVS)." · "Tools cannot comprehensively detect, test,
or protect against the OWASP Top 10." · "OWASP discourages any claims of full coverage
of the OWASP Top 10, because it's simply untrue."

**Top 10:2025 (vocabulary):** A01 Broken Access Control · A02 Security Misconfiguration
· A03 Software Supply Chain Failures · A04 Cryptographic Failures · A05 Injection ·
A06 Insecure Design · A07 Authentication Failures · A08 Software or Data Integrity
Failures · A09 Security Logging & Alerting Failures · A10 Mishandling of Exceptional
Conditions.

**PortSwigger:** "If a user can gain access to functionality that they are not permitted
to access then this is vertical privilege escalation." · "Horizontal privilege escalation
occurs if a user is able to gain access to resources belonging to another user, instead
of their own resources of that type."

**WSTG checklists README:** the *Testing Checklist* sheet "facilitates simple progress
tracking against each of the 'tests' outlined in the guide"; *Risk Assessment Calculator*
is "a dropdown driven sheet for calculating likelihood and impact scores, and a
qualitative overall risk rating."
